# v3.4.0: hosted netplay and achievements, and the first big boards (MiSTer Phase F3a)

A slot in [`v3.1-to-v4.0-line-plan.md`](v3.1-to-v4.0-line-plan.md), drafted on
2026-10-07. The codename is asked at the cut.

**Carried in from v3.3.0 (2026-10-16).** v3.3.0 shipped without its MiSTer
Phase F2 rows (the slot-scheduled SDRAM arbiter, DDR3, save states, rewind,
`hps_io` under Verilator, the headline switch) and without wgpu 31 / egui 0.37;
the maintainer moved them here rather than renumber the line. Their rows,
gates and risks are as written in [`v3.3.0-plan.md`](v3.3.0-plan.md) rows 1-6
and 10, and they come before this slot's own items. If the combined scope does
not fit, F2 keeps priority over F3a, as F2 is what F3a's large boards need.

**Decisions taken at the start (maintainer, 2026-10-16; D30-D32 in the line
plan).** The hosted services run on the maintainer's Cloudflare account at a
`*.workers.dev` subdomain, operated by the maintainer (D30, which settles the
line plan's open v3.4.0 question). The live RetroAchievements steps (the browser
login and unlock, and sending the hardcore application) are the maintainer's,
with their own account; until they report back those two gates stay open (D31).
The privacy policy is `PRIVACY.md` in the repository and a page on GitHub Pages
(D32).

**Measured before starting (2026-10-16).** crates.io's newest wgpu is 30.0.1:
**wgpu 31 is not released**, so row 10's wgpu half is blocked upstream. egui,
egui-wgpu and egui-winit 0.37.0 are released and require wgpu `^30.0` and winit
`^0.30.13`, so row 10 becomes egui 0.37 on wgpu 30, with the vendored
`egui-winit` re-diffed or removed. **Row 10 status: egui 0.37 DONE** (merge `0f201a9f`: the vendored
`egui-winit` removed after a wasm32 build of the registry crate passed; the
emoji fonts kept by a feature egui 0.37 split out, pinned by a glyph test);
**wgpu 31 BLOCKED**, unreleased upstream.

**Release notes, both repositories: open with the build switch.** From
v3.4.0 MiSTer's updater installs the OFF-DIE build as `RustyNES`, and it needs
the SDRAM add-on; a board without one must pick `RustyNES_Lite` from the menu.
The notes say so first, with the SDRAM pin constraints still provisional and
no hardware run of either build. They also name the one off-die difference that
remains, attributed: *Ganbare Goemon Gaiden* changes a CHR bank in the middle
of a background fetch, two master clocks before the PPU latches the byte;
block RAM answers in time and SDRAM cannot, so eight pixels at the right edge
of line 131 show the old bank. What the board does is not established
(`T-MISTER-OFFDIE-CHR-BANKWRITE`, `T-MISTER-WRITE-COMMIT-POINT`).

**The deploy moves to v3.4.1 (maintainer, 2026-10-17).** The maintainer is away with limited connectivity, so v3.4.0 ships the service code undeployed: no workflow sets `RUSTYNES_HOSTED_BASE`, the web glue's `HOSTED_RA_PROXY_BASE` is empty, and `a_stock_build_has_no_hosted_base` pins the native default, so no build points at a hosted base. v3.4.1 deploys it, with the three steps below done by the maintainer first, and with the RA limits as proposed (240 / 10 / 50,000), re-checked against the first month's traffic.

**Before the hosted Worker is deployed (maintainer).** The deploy waits for the
release PR to be green and for the maintainer's Cloudflare budget alert,
`wrangler login` and the TURN key. The RA proxy limits that #613's review added
were chosen, not measured: `RA_PER_IP_PER_HOUR` 240, `RA_LOGINS_PER_IP_PER_HOUR`
10, `RA_DAILY_CAP` 50,000 (all `wrangler.jsonc` vars). A playing rcheevos
session sends a keep-alive about every two minutes (about 30 requests an hour)
plus its logins and unlocks, which makes 240 an hour roughly six or seven
concurrent players behind one shared address (a school, a carrier NAT), and the
daily cap on the order of 1,600 player-hours, after which browser achievements
stop for everyone until the next UTC day. These are estimates from the client's
design, to be confirmed or changed by the maintainer at deploy time and
re-checked against the first month's traffic.

**Open before the cut: the egui 0.37 frame-time A/B.** One relative frame-time
A/B of the egui merge read +66%; a repeat on the same busy host read the
opposite sign. That fits host contention, but it does not prove it. The cut
waits on two independent `scripts/perf/ab_check.sh` runs on a quiet host
(no ladder, Quartus or cargo job running) and the release PR's CI frame-time
job. A consistent slowdown is a regression to fix or record before release,
not noise.
