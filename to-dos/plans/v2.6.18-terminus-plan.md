# v2.6.18 "Terminus" — a CPU write lands where the cycle ENDS, and this core applies it where the cycle begins

> **SHIPPED AS v2.6.17 "Terminus".** This plan was written when the write-placement work was scoped as its own release, v2.6.18. The move was refuted (see *CLOSED* below), so what remained was not a scheduler change needing its own ADR and re-baseline, and it was folded into v2.6.17. The filename keeps its original version because `CHANGELOG.md`, `VERSION-PLAN.md` and `docs/accuracy-ledger.md` all link to this path; **the release that contains this work is v2.6.17**, and every "v2.6.18" below should be read as naming the plan, not a release.

> Covers exactly one release. Opened by a measurement taken during v2.6.17,
> recorded in that release's CHANGELOG under *Measured and rejected*.

## What opens the version: two dots, measured twice, on a ROM that names the dots

AccuracyCoin `Frozen OAM2 Increment` states the dots it writes on, which makes
the alignment directly checkable rather than inferable. Probing both `$2001`
writes the test performs, armed to the single frame the behaviour occurs in:

| write | the ROM names | this core applies |
|---|---|---|
| disable `$00` | scanline 194, dot 242 | scanline 194, dot **240** |
| enable `$1E` | dot 256 | dot **254** |

Both exactly **two dots early**, on two independent writes in one run.
> **RETRACTED — see *SETTLED* below.** The gap is HALF A DOT (2 master
> clocks); this figure compared a write-instant dot against the ROM's effect
> dot. Kept in place because it is the premise the version was scoped on.

The mechanism is not in dispute and is not a tuning constant. `Cpu::start_cycle`
catches the PPU up **before** the bus access, so a PPU register write is
applied at **M2-low** — the first of the CPU cycle's three PPU dots. A 6502
commits a write at **phi2**, the third. Everything downstream of a `$2001`,
`$2000`, `$2005`, `$2006` or `$4014` write therefore observes it two dots
earlier than hardware does.

**This is v2.5.4's finding one layer out.** That release found the
co-simulation TESTBENCH presenting every CPU access on the second of a cycle's
three dots and moved it to phi2; the EMULATOR applies them on the first, and
nothing has re-measured that since the v2.0.0 scheduler rewrite.

## Why this is a version and not a patch

The tempting fix was tried in v2.6.17 and is **refuted, with numbers**. Delaying
the rendering-enable gate (`rendering_enabled_delayed`, shipped lag 1) was swept
1..4 against the full battery with the mask-timing-sensitive tests as controls:

| lag | passed | Frozen OAM2 | Stale Sprite Shift Regs | Misaligned OAM2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 (shipped)** | **143/144** | FAIL | pass | pass |
| 2 | 141/144 | FAIL | FAIL | FAIL |
| 3 | 141/144 | FAIL | FAIL | FAIL |
| 4 | 140/144 | FAIL | FAIL | FAIL |

No value closes the test and every value above the shipped one regresses two
tests that pass today. **The gate is not the lever; the write placement is.**

Moving it shifts every PPU register write in every game. That reaches the
60-ROM commercial oracle, the visual baseline corpus, every committed golden
whose run contains a PPU register write, and the co-simulation suite in the
sibling. It is ADR 0029 territory and needs its own re-baselining, which is
precisely why it is scoped here instead of appended to v2.6.17.

## Deliverable

- **A. Establish the control BEFORE changing anything.** Capture the current
  per-cycle bus stream and the framebuffer/audio hashes for the corpus that
  will be re-baselined. The first difference after the change must be a PPU
  register write's effect dot and nothing else; anything earlier means the
  experiment changed rather than the write placement.

- **B. Move the write to phi2**, behind a default-off feature so the shipped
  build is byte-identical until the evidence says otherwise. The change is in
  the `start_cycle` / `end_cycle` ordering in `rustynes-core`, not in the PPU.

- **C. Measure, per test, with controls named.** AccuracyCoin by test name (not
  the headline count — v2.6.17 showed the count hides which tests moved),
  nestest 0-diff, blargg batteries, `cpu_interrupts_v2`, the commercial oracle,
  the visual corpus.

- **D. Decide on the evidence, and accept a refutation.** If phi2 placement is
  a net regression, that is a result: revert, keep the numbers, and record why
  the M2-low placement is load-bearing. This project has shipped rejected
  changes with their measurements before and this plan authorises it again.

- **E. If it lands: the ADR.** ADR 0029 states the scheduler's access model;
  moving the commit point amends it, and the amendment names what was
  re-baselined and why.

## The gate

- **`Frozen OAM2 Increment` is NOT the acceptance criterion.** It is one
  observation. Closing it while regressing anything else is a failure, and the
  v2.6.17 sweep is the worked example of why a single-test target misleads.
- Per-test AccuracyCoin comparison before and after, **by name**. Net
  improvement with zero named regressions, or the change does not land.
- nestest 0-diff, blargg 11/11, `cpu_interrupts_v2` 5/5 — all VERIFIED.
- Every re-baselined golden accompanied by the reason its bytes moved. A
  re-baseline with no stated cause is how a real regression gets blessed.
- The default build byte-identical while the feature is off, measured.

## Progress: the diagnosis is CONFIRMED, and the cost is three tests

Deliverable B is built (`phi2-write-sweep`, default off) and C's first sweep is
done. `write_split`'s `pre` is the lever, and the result is unambiguous:

| offset | commit lands at | passed | gained | lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 (shipped)** | 1.5 dots in | **143/144** | — | — |
| 1 | 1.75 dots in | 143/144 | — | — |
| **2** | **2.0 dots = phi2** | 141/144 | **Frozen OAM2 Increment** | Arbitrary Sprite zero, Stale Sprite Shift Regs, Misaligned OAM2 Address |
| 3 | past phi2 | 141/144 | Frozen OAM2 Increment | the same three |
| 4 | past phi2 | 141/144 | Frozen OAM2 Increment | the same three |

Two things fall out, and the second is the version's real subject.

**The root cause is confirmed.** Offset 2 is exactly where the commit crosses
into the cycle's third dot — phi2 — and that is the offset at which
`Frozen OAM2 Increment` starts passing. Offset 1 changes nothing at all,
because it does not cross the dot boundary. So the test is not sensitive to
"a bit later"; it is sensitive to *which dot the write commits on*, which is
the hypothesis, stated in advance, behaving as predicted.

**Three tests currently depend on the WRONG placement.** `Arbitrary Sprite
zero`, `Stale Sprite Shift Regs` and `Misaligned OAM2 Address` pass today and
fail at phi2. That is the v2.5.7 shape exactly — a wrong phase with windows
downstream compensating for it — and it means the version cannot be "move the
write and re-baseline". Each of the three has to be examined on its own terms
to establish whether it is a compensation that must move with the phase, or a
genuine behaviour the phi2 model gets wrong.

Note `Misaligned OAM2 Address` among them: v2.6.17 closed it by modelling
OAM2Address as a live counter, and that model was calibrated against the
M2-low write placement. It is the clearest candidate for a compensation.

**Nothing is adopted.** The shipped default stays offset 0 and the build is
byte-identical with the feature off (the `const` path is retained under
`cfg(not(feature))`, so the hot `write_split` pays no atomic load). 141 < 143,
so adopting now would be a net regression blessed by a single-test target —
precisely what this plan's gate forbids.

## Is `Misaligned OAM2 Address` a compensation? YES — and it is a SUB-DOT one

Asked directly, and answered by measurement rather than by argument. The ROM
stores the byte it read from `$2004` at zero page `$50`, and that test is the
LAST catalog entry, so the byte survives a full battery run and can be read
with no instrumentation at all:

| configuration | `$0050` | OAM2Address reached |
|---|---|---|
| shipped | `$06` | **`$18`** — the value the ROM expects |
| write +2 | `$A3` | **`$17`** — exactly one short |
| read +2, write +2 | `$A3` | `$17` |

`$A3` is OAM2 index `$17`; `$06` is index `$18`. Moving the write costs
**exactly one increment**.

**Two hypotheses were killed on the way to the answer.**

*It is not read/write spacing.* The first sweep moved writes alone, which
changes the gap between a write and a following read rather than moving the
access model as a unit — a real flaw in that experiment. But at read +2 /
write +2, where the spacing is preserved, the test still fails with the same
`$A3`. The failure tracks the WRITE offset alone.

*It is not the dot arithmetic.* Running the implementation's own rule —
increment on even dots in 256..=320 while rendering is enabled — over each
placement gives **24 increments (`$18`) in all three cases**, including the
dots the ROM itself names. The window is 18 dots wide with 9 even dots inside
it either way. The counter's dot model is placement-INDEPENDENT and correct.

**So what is calibrated is the sub-dot boundary.** `tick_oam_bus` is called
once per dot, gated on `mask.rendering_enabled()`. A `$2001` write mutates
that mask partway THROUGH a dot, so whether the boundary dot counts depends on
whether the write landed before or after that dot's tick. At the shipped
M2-low placement the boundary falls the right way; move the commit two dots
and it flips, costing one increment.

**What this means for the version, and it is good news.** The v2.6.17
OAM2Address counter does not need redesigning — its arithmetic is already
independent of where the write lands. What needs deriving is the ordering
between a mask write and the per-dot OAM2 tick within a single dot. That is a
much smaller and better-defined job than "recalibrate the counter", and it is
the same class of question v2.5.4 answered for the co-simulation testbench.

## Reads must NOT simply move to phi2

Measured while answering the above, and it changes the plan's shape. Moving
the READ sample point to dot 2.0 (offset +4) does not merely fail to help — it
costs **six NMI tests** (`NMI Overlap BRK`, `NMI Overlap IRQ`, `NMI Timing`,
`NMI Suppression`, `NMI at VBlank end`, `NMI disabled at VBlank`), drops the
battery to **137/144**, and UNDOES the `Frozen OAM2 Increment` gain that the
write move buys.

The read sample point is doing real work for NMI and VBlank timing. "A 6502
samples reads at phi2 too, so move both" is the naive reading and the numbers
refuse it. Whatever the final model is, it is not one offset applied to both
halves of the access.

## The sub-dot ordering: located exactly, and one fix attempt refuted

The ordering question is now answered precisely. Instrumenting which dots the
OAM2 maintenance actually sees rendering enabled on, for the misaligned
scanline:

| placement | disabled dots | even dots inside | counter ends at |
|---|---|---|---|
| shipped (writes land 266 / 284) | **[267 … 285]** | 9 | `$18` — correct |
| write +2 (writes land 268 / 286) | **[268 … 286]** | **10** | `$17` — one short |

Both windows are **19 dots wide**. What differs is their PARITY: the first
starts and ends on odd dots, the second on even ones, so the second contains
one extra even dot and the counter loses exactly one increment. The ROM's own
window — disable ON 268, enable ON 286 — is 18 dots, [268..=285], with 9 even
dots, which is the correct `$18`.

So the defect is not the counter and not the window length. **The write's
effect lands one dot late on the trailing edge**, and how late depends on
where the write's master clock falls inside a dot, which is exactly what the
commit-point move changes.

### Attempt 1: quantise the gate to a dot boundary — REFUTED

Latched the `$2001` value at write time together with `self.dot`, and applied
it from the next dot boundary, so the OAM2 maintenance would stop reading the
live mask and the window's parity would follow the dot the write landed on
rather than sub-dot rounding. Predicted `$18` at both placements.

Measured: `$06` at the shipped placement (unchanged) and **`$A3` at write +2**
— no change at all. The latch does not decouple them, because `self.dot` at
write time is itself a function of how far `run_ppu_to` has advanced, so the
quantisation inherits the same rounding it was meant to remove. Reverted; the
prediction and its refutation are the result.

**This is the fourth attempt on this behaviour** (freeze `base = 0`,
rendering-enable lag, write commit offset, gate quantisation), which is this
project's own threshold for questioning the approach instead of trying a
fifth. The next move is not another patch at the OAM2 gate: it is to decide
what the dot grid means for a write at all — i.e. whether `run_ppu_to` should
advance to a dot BOUNDARY before applying an access, so that "the dot a write
happened on" is well defined independently of the commit offset. That is a
scheduler question, which is the version this plan is for.

## Deliverable A is DONE — and the obvious instrument was rejected first

`crates/rustynes-test-harness/tests/terminus_control.rs` captures the
first-difference control on the SHIPPED scheduler, and is demonstrated by
mutation to catch the exact change this version will make.

**The co-simulation checkpoint chain was built first and thrown away.** It is
the obvious instrument — a rolling per-cycle FNV at 16 bytes per 4096 cycles,
with `first_full_capture_difference` to localise — and a corpus of four
workloads was exported and committed. Then the mutation pass: moving the write
commit two master clocks produced a **byte-identical chain**. Not a weak
signal; no signal at all.

The reason is structural and was worth the detour to find. Those chains need
`--irq-trace`, `irq-timing-trace` selects a different `for sub_dot in 0..3`
loop in `Bus::tick_one_cpu_cycle` (the v2.4.1 finding), and that loop does not
go through `write_split`. **The instrument is blind to the subject of this
version by construction** — a gate that would have reported "no change" while
the scheduler moved underneath it. Deleted rather than kept with a caveat.

The replacement runs the DEFAULT feature set and hashes what a user observes
(pre-palette framebuffer + work RAM, per frame, rolling FNV-1a).
`TERMINUS_DUMP=<dir>` writes the per-frame chain so the earliest differing
frame can be read off.

**Measured coverage, stated rather than implied.** Under the phi2 mutation:

| workload | moves? |
|---|---|
| `nestest` (400 frames) | **yes** — the control is not blind |
| `ppu_vbl_nmi/01-vbl_basics` | no, at 60 frames or 400 |
| `sprite_hit_tests/01.basics` | no, at 60 frames or 400 |

The second half is a finding, not a gap. Those two workloads are measurably
INSENSITIVE to a two-master-clock shift in the write commit, so the corpus
needing re-baseline when the dot grid moves is narrower than "everything with
a PPU write in it". Knowing which workloads do NOT move is what keeps a
re-baseline from being blessed wholesale.

The depth is also a measurement: at 60 frames only `nestest` moved because the
blargg ROMs were still initialising, which is the "stimulus blind" outcome in
this project's taxonomy. 400 frames is the remediation; `AccuracyCoin` was
dropped rather than lengthened, since reaching its battery needs a START press
and thousands of frames that `accuracycoin.rs` already drives.

So A is satisfied and the dot grid can move: any change now has a baseline to
be judged against, on the scheduler that actually ships.

The write commit was moved to phi2 and diffed against the control. Deliverable

| 4 | 0 | 0 |

### The decision, against the gate this plan wrote in advance

The gate says: *net improvement with zero named regressions, or the change
does not land.* Net is **-2 on AccuracyCoin plus an independent-oracle
regression**, so **it does not land**.

The distinction that matters for whoever picks this up: the six framebuffer
goldens are BASELINES — if phi2 is right they legitimately move and get
re-blessed. `ppu_vbl_nmi/10-even_odd_timing` is not a baseline. It is a
third-party ROM with its own pass/fail verdict, and it went from pass to
`09`. That is evidence against naive phi2 placement, not a golden awaiting a
re-bless, and it is the single most important number here.

So the position is: phi2 is the right DIAGNOSIS (it closes the test the whole
line started from, and the first difference is clean) and the wrong CHANGE as
applied — moving the write alone, with reads left at dot 1.0 and the three
dependent behaviours un-rederived. At least one of those three is proven to be
a sub-dot compensation. The even-odd frame timing regression suggests a
fourth interaction nobody has looked at yet.

Next, and not before: re-derive `10-even_odd_timing` under phi2 from the ROM's
own statement of what it measures, since that is an independent oracle and
therefore adjudicates. The three AccuracyCoin compensations come after.

## `10-even_odd_timing` re-derived under phi2 — the oracle regression CLOSES

The ROM states its own verdict, which is where this started rather than ended.
Under phi2 it reports:

> `Failed #2` — **"Clock is skipped too soon, relative to enabling BG"**

### The codebase already contained the compensation, and says so

`Ppu::mask_for_skip_check` is a two-stage delay pipeline consumed by nothing
except the pre-render dot-339 odd-frame skip. Its own doc comment names the
cause exactly:

> "enough to compensate for the lockstep bus model applying `cpu_write` at the
> *start* of a CPU cycle (before the cycle's 3 PPU ticks) while real hardware
> latches the write at φ2 (effectively the *end* of the cycle)."

So a previous author independently diagnosed this version's root cause and
worked around it LOCALLY, for one detector, years before it was named
globally. That is the strongest evidence yet that phi2 is the right model:
the workaround exists, it is documented, and it describes the same defect.

Under phi2 the compensation becomes a DOUBLE correction, which is precisely
what "too soon, relative to enabling BG" reports.

### The re-derivation is a HALVING, not a removal

Predicted: remove the compensation entirely. **Wrong, and measured wrong** —
at zero stages the ROM flips to `Failed #3` "skipped too **late**", at both
placements. The correct value is bracketed, and it is one stage:

| skip-check delay | shipped write | phi2 write |
|---|---|---|
| 2 stages (shipped) | **pass** `08 08 09 07` | `#2` too soon |
| 1 stage | `#3` too late | **pass** `08 08 09 07` |
| 0 stages | `#3` too late | `#3` too late |

At one stage under phi2 the ROM prints the SAME four values as the shipped
configuration does today. Not merely a pass — the same measurement.

### What that leaves

Re-running everything at phi2 + one stage: **85 binaries ok, 3 failed**
(against 83 / 4 for naive phi2).

* `ppu_vbl_nmi/10-even_odd_timing` — **no longer failing.** The only
  ADJUDICATING regression is gone.
* AccuracyCoin — still 141/144: gains `Frozen OAM2 Increment`, still loses
  `Misaligned OAM2 Address`, `Stale Sprite Shift Regs`, `Arbitrary Sprite
  zero`. Those three are the remaining compensations.
* Six framebuffer baselines still move, which is bookkeeping if phi2 is right.

The gate still is not met — net AccuracyCoin is -2 — so it still does not
land. But the blocker that mattered is removed: what remains are three
compensations of the kind already proven to exist, not an independent oracle
saying the model is wrong.

## `Arbitrary Sprite zero` and `Stale Sprite Shift Regs` — read, and NOT closed

Both were read at the source and one hypothesis was tested and refuted. What
they need is recorded; what they are not is guesswork.

### What each actually asserts

`Stale Sprite Shift Regs` fails at **test 5**, which the ROM titles *"What if
we prepare the sprite counter but don't let the counter activate on dot 339?"*
and explains in full:

> "If the ppu is rendering on dot 339, then the shifter counters are set to
> 'counting'. If rendering was not enabled on dot 339, the shifter counters
> will be in whatever state they were previously in, which is likely
> 'halted'."

It is a second **dot-339 rendering gate**, alongside the odd-frame skip.
The ROM even states the write delay it assumes: `STA $2001` "begins on
scanline 3, dot 325. Accounting for the delay, rendering should be disabled
around dot 334 or 335" — i.e. the commit lands in the instruction's LAST
cycle, roughly nine dots after it starts. That is a direct, independent
statement of the phi2 model from the test author.

`Arbitrary Sprite zero` fails at **test 3**, *"Misaligned OAM can properly
draw a sprite, and yes, it can even trigger a sprite zero hit"* — sprite
evaluation with `OAMADDR` not a multiple of four. Its code is shared with
`Misaligned OAM behavior` and `$2004 behavior`, so the three move together.

### The hypothesis that was tried, and refuted

At dot 339 this core has TWO rendering gates with DIFFERENT delays: the
sprite-counter re-arm reads `rendering_enabled_delayed` (one stage), the
odd-frame skip reads `mask_for_skip_check` (two stages). Since the skip needed
2 -> 1 under phi2, the natural hypothesis was that every gate loses one stage,
so the counter re-arm should go 1 -> 0.

Implemented (moving the re-arm out of the `rendering_gate` block onto the live
mask) and measured: **all three still fail, unchanged**. And the reason it was
inert is visible in the ROM's own setup — test 5 holds rendering DISABLED
across dot 339, so a gate on the live mask skips the block exactly as the
one-stage gate did. The knob was never on the path the test exercises.

### Where this stops, and why

That is a third refuted attempt in this family (gate quantisation, one-stage
-less, and before them the rendering-enable lag). The threshold for
questioning the approach rather than reaching for a fourth guess has been
passed again, and the honest next step is a PROBE rather than a patch:
instrument the dot at which rendering actually transitions for test 5's two
`$2001` writes, at both placements, the way the OAM2 window was localised.
That measurement is cheap, it is what settled the OAM2 question, and it will
say whether the disable is landing before or after 339 in each configuration
instead of leaving it to be inferred.

Nothing was adopted. The tree is unchanged and AccuracyCoin holds 143/144.

## The probe, the README, and TWO ERRORS THAT WERE CANCELLING

The probe settled the write-placement question, the upstream README supplied a
behaviour this core was missing, and together they change the verdict on phi2.

### The probe: both placements are INSIDE the ROM's stated window

Logged every `$2001` write on the isolating sub-test ROM
(`ppu-misc-stale-sprite-shift-regs.nes`), which removes the battery's other
traffic entirely. Test 5's pair:

| placement | disable lands | enable lands |
|---|---|---|
| shipped | scanline 3, dot **334** | scanline 4, dot **161** |
| phi2 | scanline 3, dot **335** | scanline 4, dot **162** |

The ROM states its own expectation: the disable "should be disabled around dot
334 or 335", the enable "around dot 161 or 162". **Both placements land inside
both windows.**

> **Observation-point note (added when this was re-measured).** A later probe
> using `ppu-state-trace`, whose hook reads state at the END of each dot, reports
> every one of these numbers ONE HIGHER (shipped 335 / 162, phi2 336 / 163). The
> two are the same event seen from either side of a dot boundary, and the shape
> -- both placements one dot apart, shipped inside the ROM's window -- is
> identical. Quote the instrument with the number; the bare dot is ambiguous by
> exactly one, which is the whole of the 241-vs-240 confusion. So the write placement is not what decides this test, and the
"two dots early" framing from the earlier Frozen OAM2 probe does not
generalise — +2 master clocks is half a dot, which crosses a dot boundary
only sometimes.

Also measured and refuted: an alignment fragility. Across **eight power-on
seeds** the verdict byte at `$048F` is `01` (pass) for every seed at the
shipped placement and `16` (fail, code 5) for every seed at phi2. Deterministic
on the placement, not on alignment.

### The README: an increment this core never performed

AccuracyCoin's README documents `Frozen OAM2 Increment` test 3 as:

> "If the OAM2 address overflows for any reason, including the end of sprite
> fetch where **the address is incremented on dot 321**"

This core increments `OAM2Address` on dots 256..=320 only. Adding dot 321 —
subject to the freeze flag, as the same sentence implies — and the arithmetic
closes exactly against the dot-window measurement taken earlier:

| placement | increments 256..320 | + dot 321 | reads | expected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| shipped | 24 | **25 = `$19`** | `$C1` | `$06` |
| phi2 | 23 | **24 = `$18`** | `$06` | `$06` |

**So `Misaligned OAM2 Address` was passing on the shipped build because two
errors cancelled**: a missing dot-321 increment (one too few) against a write
landing a dot early (one extra even dot inside the disabled window). Each is
wrong; together they produced the right byte. That is this project's
"two traces agreeing was two errors cancelling" pattern, and only a documented
third fact could expose it.

### Where the combination stands

phi2 + dot-321 increment + the odd-frame skip at one stage:

| configuration | AccuracyCoin | failing binaries |
|---|---|---|
| baseline (shipped) | **143/144** | — |
| naive phi2 | 141/144 | 4, incl. the blargg oracle |
| + skip re-derived | 141/144 | 3 |
| **+ dot-321 increment** | **142/144** | 3 |

Still one short of the baseline, so it still does not land — but the two
regressions that remain are `Stale Sprite Shift Regs` and `Arbitrary Sprite
zero`, and `Misaligned OAM2 Address` has moved from a compensation to a
correctly-derived pass.

### The next lead, from the same README

`Arbitrary Sprite zero` test 3 is "Misaligned OAM should be able to trigger a
sprite zero hit", and the README's adjacent `Misaligned OAM Behavior` entries
give the mechanics it rests on — including a rule this core should be checked
against:

> "Misaligned OAM should stay misaligned until an object's Y position is out
> of the range of this scanline, at which point the OAM address is incremented
> by 4 and bitwise ANDed with `$FC`" — and, if secondary OAM is full at that
> moment, "you should instead only increment the OAM address by 5."

That is the same shape as the dot-321 finding: a specific, documented
behaviour to check the implementation against rather than a timing knob to
sweep. Do that before touching any gate.

### Research note

The nesdev wiki does NOT document the sprite shifter counter's
halted/counting state at dot 339 at all; `PPU rendering` and `PPU sprite
evaluation` cover the shift-register reloads and the odd-frame skip and stop
there. For this behaviour AccuracyCoin's own prose IS the specification, which
is why rung 2 of the provenance ladder was exhausted without result here.

## The misaligned-OAM increment rules, checked against the implementation

Both rules from AccuracyCoin's README `Misaligned OAM Behavior` entry were
checked against the sprite-evaluation FSM. **One is implemented, one is not.**

The FSM splits `OAMADDR` into `sprite_eval_n` (sprite) and `sprite_eval_m`
(byte), seeded from `oam_addr` at eval start, so the misalignment lives in `m`
and the rules translate directly.

| README rule | this core | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| secondary OAM FULL, Y out of range: "only increment the OAM address by 5" | `m = (m+1) & 3; n += 1` in `sprite_eval_overflow_search` — the buggy n+m increment | **correct** |
| NOT full, Y out of range: "incremented by 4 and bitwise ANDed with `$FC`" | `n += 1`, **`m` left untouched** | **MISMATCH** |

The second is a real, documented divergence. Clearing `m` is what `& $FC`
means, and this core carries the misaligned byte index forward instead. It is
invisible whenever `OAMADDR` is a multiple of four — `m` is already 0 at every
y-test — so only misaligned OAM can observe it.

### But no gate can adjudicate it, and that was measured

Applying the rule changes NOTHING: 143/144 at the shipped placement,
142/144 in the phi2 + dot-321 combination, the same tests failing in both.

That is not because the path is unreached. Instrumented over a battery run,
the out-of-range branch fires **56,953,944 times**, of which **114 have
`m != 0`** — the only case the rule changes. So the corrected behaviour
happens 114 times per run and no test verdict moves, INCLUDING AccuracyCoin's
own `Misaligned OAM behavior`, which exists for exactly this area.

**Not adopted at the time of that check.** The rule is documented and the code
contradicted it, which is an argument for changing it; but this project's bar
is that an accuracy change is pinned by a test that fails without it, and none
did. Shipping it then would have been an unverifiable behaviour change to the
core, and "no test covers it" is not evidence either way.

### Settled: the stimulus was written, and then the rule was adopted

What that section said would settle it — "a stimulus that reaches those 114
cases and asserts on them" — is now
`misaligned_oam_out_of_range_advance_follows_both_rules` in
`crates/rustynes-ppu/src/ppu.rs`. It drives one y-test from `OAMADDR = $05`
(`n = 1`, `m = 1`) past an out-of-range Y, at dot 0 / 65 / 66, and asserts the
resulting `(n, m)` for **both** rules in the README entry:

| secondary OAM | rule | expected `(n, m)` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| not full | `OAMADDR += 4`, then `& $FC` | `(2, 0)` |
| full | `OAMADDR += 5` | `(2, 2)` |

Against the unmodified code it fails on the first assertion —
`left: (2, 1)`, `right: (2, 0)`: `n` advanced and the misaligned byte index
was carried forward rather than cleared. That is the mutation demonstration
and it is the whole argument for adopting the rule, since the battery is
**143/144 with the identical failing set before and after**. The test is the
only thing in the project that can see this behaviour; it is also now the only
thing that has to.

Note what the test does NOT establish. It pins the rule as AccuracyCoin's
README states it, on one misalignment (`m = 1`) at one dot pair. It is not an
independent measurement against silicon, and no ROM in this corpus
discriminates the two behaviours — which is exactly why the 114-occurrence
count was measured before the change was made rather than after.

## CLOSED: the compensation stays, and v2.6.18 ships at 143/144

**Maintainer decision, 2026-09-11: phi2 write placement is NOT adopted.** The
`mask_for_skip_check` two-stage delay pipeline stays exactly where it is, and
this version closes with AccuracyCoin at **143 of 144 (99.31%)** — the number
it has held since `ba26eef0`.

This is the outcome the plan authorised in advance ("Refutation is authorised
up front"), so it is a result rather than a shortfall. What the version
delivers is the measurement, the instrument that produced it, and one accuracy
fix that came out of the investigation sideways.

### Why not adopted, in one table

| combination | AccuracyCoin | independent oracle |
|---|---|---|
| shipped (M2-low + the 2-stage compensation) | **143/144** | clean |
| phi2, write alone | 141/144 | `ppu_vbl_nmi/10-even_odd_timing` FAILS `09` |
| phi2 + dot-321 + `skip(1)` | 142/144 | clean |

The best phi2 combination found is still **net −1** against what ships, and it
costs a save-state epoch plus a re-baseline of six framebuffer goldens. The
argument for taking it anyway is that phi2 is the correct DIAGNOSIS — a 6502
commits a write at the end of its cycle, and `mask_for_skip_check` is an
explicit, self-documenting compensation for not doing that. The argument
against is that the diagnosis being right does not make this CHANGE right:
three dependent behaviours are proven or suspected compensations, only two of
them have been re-derived, and `Arbitrary Sprite zero` / `Stale Sprite Shift
Regs` are still open at the point the version ends.

Adopting a mechanism while two of its dependants are un-re-derived is how a
compensation gets replaced by a differently-shaped compensation, which is
strictly worse than the one that is at least documented as one.

### What is NOT retracted

The diagnosis stands and is the version's main finding. Specifically:

- **The write placement is off by HALF A DOT** (originally written as "two
  dots early" — RETRACTED, see *SETTLED*). Measured on two independent
  `$2001` writes against a ROM that names the dots it writes on — 242 and 256,
  where this core applies 240 and 254. That measurement is not in dispute and
  is not affected by the decision not to move.
- **`mask_for_skip_check` is a compensation, and it says so in its own
  comment.** Under phi2 it needs ONE stage rather than two, which was derived
  from the ROM's own statement and produces the identical `08 08 09 07`.
- **`Misaligned OAM2 Address` was passing via two cancelling errors**, found
  because the README documents an increment on dot 321 that this core did not
  model. Both errors are now fixed independently, so it passes for the right
  reason rather than by coincidence — that is `ba26eef0` + `a437b276` and it
  is v2.6.17's content, unaffected by this decision.

### What ships as v2.6.18

1. **The misaligned-OAM `+4 & $FC` realignment** (`381604ce`) — the one core
   behaviour change, pinned by a test that fails without it, reached 114 times
   per battery run and invisible to every ROM in the corpus.
2. **`terminus_control.rs`** — the first-difference control, a standing gate on
   the DEFAULT feature set over three workloads at 400 frames. Built for this
   experiment; it outlives it, because "the first divergence must be the cycle
   you aimed at" is the check every future timing change needs.
3. **The sweepable write-commit dot** (`phi2-write-sweep`, default-off,
   `const fn` when the feature is absent) plus `phi2sweep.rs` — kept so the
   next attempt re-measures in an afternoon instead of rebuilding the
   apparatus. The default build is byte-identical with the feature off.
4. **The measurement itself**, recorded here with every number, including the
   three rejected/refuted attempts.

### What carries forward, and the condition for reopening

`Frozen OAM2 Increment` stays in `KNOWN_FAILING` as the single known failure.
Reopening the alignment question needs THREE things first, in this order, and
none of them needs the write to move to be worked on:

1. `Arbitrary Sprite zero` test 3 and `Stale Sprite Shift Regs` test 5
   re-derived under phi2 the way `10-even_odd_timing` was — from each ROM's
   own statement of what it measures, not from the delta.
2. A decision on reads, which this plan explicitly did NOT settle: they must
   not simply move to phi2, and the offset was measured on writes only.
3. The sibling's co-simulation goldens re-exported from a pinned oracle
   commit, since a change in oracle behaviour re-opens every gate that
   compares against it.

Only after all three does phi2 become a change rather than a hypothesis with
an apparatus attached.

### The sibling is pinned, so its gates did NOT re-open — and that is a debt

Risk 4 in this plan said an oracle change re-opens every co-simulation gate
that compares against it. Checked rather than assumed:
`RustyNES_MiSTer/tb/ORACLE_COMMIT` pins the oracle at **`957a63af`**, the
v2.6.14 cut, so the sibling's goldens were exported from an oracle that
predates every PPU change in this release and its gates are unaffected. Nothing
in the sibling needs to move for v2.6.17 to ship.

**That is a deferral, not an absence.** Three landed changes alter PPU
behaviour the goldens capture — the `OAM2Address` live counter, the frozen
fetch reading index 0 for every byte, and the misaligned-OAM `+4 & $FC`
realignment — so the next bump of `ORACLE_COMMIT` past this release SHOULD move
goldens, and a DUT that has never seen these behaviours may fail gates it
passes today.

Expect that, and treat it as the pin doing its job rather than as a regression:
the first divergence must be attributable to one of those three, or something
else moved. A pin whose gates stay green while the thing it tracks has advanced
is the shape of every expired-precondition finding in this project — the
difference here is that it is written down before anyone bumps it.

## The TriCNES consult, and what it settled (2026-09-11)

The oracle's source was consulted under maintainer authorisation — TriCNES is
MIT, already vendored in-repo and attributed in `NOTICE`, so this is licence-
compatible with GPL-3.0-or-later. **Nothing was derived into the code**, so no
new attribution is owed and no region becomes an HDL black box under ADR 0037.
What it produced is understanding, recorded here so it is not re-derived.

### What TriCNES does differently

Its sprite fetch is an 8-case machine over dots 257..=320 stepping
`OAM2Address` on cases **0, 1, 2 and 7** (dots 257, 258, 259, 264 of each
group), and it keeps **one** `OAM2Address` running continuously through clear,
evaluation and fetch, every step gated on the DELAYED rendering-enable. This
core steps on every EVEN dot of 256..=320 and splits the counter in two
(`oam2_addr` for evaluation, `oam2_fetch_addr` for fetch).

Both shapes give four steps per eight-dot group, so under steady rendering they
are indistinguishable — which is why every other test passes either way.

### Three hypotheses, all REFUTED by measurement

| change | AccuracyCoin | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| adopt TriCNES's `{0,1,2,7}` fetch dots | **142/144** | worse — lost `Misaligned OAM2 Address`, did not close the target |
| gate the 63/255/339 reset on `rendering_enabled_delayed` | 143/144 | inert — no verdict moves |
| (baseline, shipped) | **143/144** | stands |

The dot-pattern port is half a port: it moves fetch stepping without the shared
counter the pattern belongs to, which is why it broke the neighbouring test.

### What the ROM's own source says, and why it is decisive

`AccuracyCoin.asm` states the rule in the test's comments:

> "This flag that prevents the OAM2 Address from incrementing (The 'OAM2
> Overflowed' flag) is cleared if **rendering is enabled** during dots 63, 255,
> and 339."

and the stimulus is explicit: disable rendering on **dot 242** of scanline 194,
re-enable on **dot 256**, so dot 255 passes with rendering off and the flag must
survive.

This core applies those `$2001` writes **two dots early** — the v2.6.17 finding —
so the re-enable lands at dot **254**, and at dot 255 rendering reads as enabled
and the reset fires. `rendering_enabled_delayed` is a **ONE-dot** delay, so it is
true at 255 as well, which is exactly why gating on it changes nothing.

**So the entry is blocked on write placement and on nothing else.** Not the dot
pattern, not the split counter, not the gate's choice of mask. That is the same
conclusion v2.6.17 reached from the other direction, now confirmed against the
ROM's own text and against an independent implementation — and it is why the
phi2 combination is the only one that closes it.

The delayed-flag gate remains a defensible candidate on its own merits (it is
what TriCNES does, and it is the ROM's wording read strictly), but no test in
this corpus discriminates it, so adopting it would breach the bar this project
set for the misaligned-OAM rule one commit earlier: an accuracy change is pinned
by a test that fails without it.

## The probe, run (2026-09-11) — and it REFUTES the leading hypothesis

The section above said the honest next step was a probe rather than a fourth
patch. It was run. `crates/rustynes-test-harness/tests/stale_sprite_shift_regs_probe.rs`
traces scanline 3, dots 300..=340 of the `ppu-misc-stale-sprite-shift-regs`

### The dot-339 gate is IDENTICAL at both placements

| placement | rendering disable | effective gate (1-dot pipeline) | vs dot 339 |
|---|---|---|---|
| shipped (M2-low) | line 3, **dot 335** | 336 | before |
| phi2 (write +2) | line 3, **dot 335** | 336 | before |

**So `Stale Sprite Shift Regs`'s phi2 regression is NOT the dot-339 rendering

## Where that leaves 144/144

- `Frozen OAM2 Increment` is gained ONLY by moving the write. Nothing else
  reaches it.
- Moving the write costs three. Two are recoverable — `Misaligned OAM2 Address`

- The last two, `Arbitrary Sprite zero` test 3 and `Stale Sprite Shift Regs`

So the ceiling with current understanding is **142/144 under phi2 against
143/144 shipped**, and the shipped configuration remains the best measured.
144/144 is not reachable today, and what stands between is not a decision about

## The three writes, measured on ONE instrument — and the trade is ONE dot

The probe was pointed at every write these tests turn on, at both placements,
comparing the dot at which PPUMASK's rendering bits actually change against the
dot the ROM's own comments name.

| write | ROM says | shipped | phi2 (+2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Frozen OAM2` disable, line 194 | "Disable rendering on dot **242**" | 241 — one EARLY | **242 — exact** |
| `Stale` t5 disable, line 3 | "disabled around dot **334 or 335**" | **335 — in range** | 336 — one late |
| `Stale` t5 enable, line 4 | "enabled around dot **161 or 162**" | **162 — in range** | 163 — one late |

This explains the battery exactly. Shipped satisfies two of the three and misses
`Frozen OAM2` by one dot: **143/144**. phi2 satisfies `Frozen OAM2` and misses
the other two by one dot: **141/144**. The knob trades one test for two.

### Two consequences, and the second is the interesting one

**No uniform offset satisfies all three.** Their tolerances sit one dot apart,
and write offsets 2 and 4 are indistinguishable on the battery, so this knob has
no intermediate setting to find. `Frozen OAM2 Increment` is therefore NOT
reachable by moving the write commit — which retires the whole approach rather
than merely costing it.

**The residual is ONE dot, not two.** That matters, because a fixed DOT offset
is the wrong shape for the thing being modelled: a 6502 commits at phi2, a fixed
point in the CPU cycle measured in MASTER CLOCKS, and which PPU dot that lands on
depends on the CPU/PPU phase alignment of the particular instruction. Three
writes at three different alignments cannot all be satisfied by one dot-quantised
constant, but they could be by a phase-exact one. That is the next hypothesis,
and it is a different hypothesis from "the write is two dots early".

### A recorded measurement I could NOT reproduce

v2.6.17 states the writes land "two dots early ... 242 and 256, where this core
applies 240 and 254", and that figure is the premise the whole phi2 line was
scoped on. **This instrument does not reproduce it**: the disable's mask
transition is at dot **241**, one dot from the ROM's 242, not two — and under
phi2 it is exactly 242.

Not asserting the earlier number was wrong; asserting that two measurements
disagree and this one is reproducible from a committed probe, with its window,
its ROM and its definition (the dot PPUMASK's rendering bits change) all stated.
The likely explanation is that the two measured different quantities — a mask
register write versus an effective rendering transition are one dot apart, which
is exactly the size of the discrepancy. **Resolve which before either figure is
quoted again**, because the two-dot version is what justified calling the
placement a defect.

## SETTLED: the gap is HALF A DOT, and "two dots early" is RETRACTED

**This CONFIRMS a finding already recorded above rather than making a new one.**
The `Arbitrary Sprite zero` / `Stale Sprite Shift Regs` section already states
that "the 'two dots early' framing from the earlier Frozen OAM2 probe does not
generalise -- +2 master clocks is half a dot, which crosses a dot boundary only
sometimes", and already reports both placements landing inside the ROM's windows
for that test. What follows adds the `Frozen OAM2` write measured on the SAME
instrument, an arithmetic derivation, and a sufficiency argument that closes it.

Saying so explicitly because the alternative is this document accreting the same
conclusion twice under two headings, which is how a corpus stops being auditable.

The 241-vs-240 discrepancy is resolved. Three independent lines agree, and the
headline figure v2.6.17 shipped is wrong as stated.

### 1. Arithmetic, from the shipped constants

`write_split(12) = (7, 5)`, and the PPU is advanced to `pre - PPU_OFFSET` =
**6 master clocks** into the CPU cycle — **1.5 dots**. phi2 is 8 master clocks,
**2.0 dots**. The gap is **2 master clocks = HALF A DOT**.

That is not an interpretation; it is the constants the shipped build compiles.

### 2. The knob that expresses phi2 is +2 master clocks

`WRITE_PHI_OFFSET` is denominated in master clocks and the value that lands the
commit on phi2 is **2**, which is the same half-dot. The sweep's own comment
says so: "read +4 and write +2 both land on dot 2.0 = phi2".

### 3. The decisive one: a half-dot nudge is SUFFICIENT to flip the test

`Frozen OAM2 Increment` fails at the shipped placement and passes at
`WRITE_PHI_OFFSET = 2`. **If the commit were two dots (8 master clocks) early, a
2-master-clock change could not possibly reach the right dot.** The test
flipping on half a dot proves the error is under one dot.

### Where 240 came from, and why 241 is what the instrument reports

`ppu-state-trace`'s hook is documented in `ppu.rs` as reading state "AFTER all
this dot's effects have applied", so a record labelled dot N is **end-of-dot N**.
Measured: PPUMASK still holds `$18` at end-of-240 and `$00` at end-of-241, so the
write lands **during dot 241**; under phi2 it lands during 242, the ROM's stated
dot.

The most likely origin of 240 is the PPU's `dot` counter read at the INSTANT of
the CPU access — `Cpu::start_cycle` catches the PPU up *before* the bus access,
so at that moment the counter has not yet completed the dot the write lands in.
That reads one lower, and comparing it against the ROM's EFFECT dot (242) makes
a one-dot modelling gap look like two.

### What this retracts, and what survives

**RETRACTED:** v2.6.17's "Both exactly two dots early, on two independent
writes", and the `240` / `254` figures in its notes, the CHANGELOG and this
plan's opening. The measurement they came from is not reproducible with an
instrument whose observation point is stated.

**SURVIVES:** the decision. v2.6.17 declined to adopt phi2 and that was correct
— now for a better reason than it gave. And the mechanism survives too: a 6502
commits at phi2 and this core commits half a dot earlier.

**WHAT CHANGES:** the shape of the remaining problem. A two-dot error is a
structural scheduler defect. A HALF-DOT error is a phase-and-rounding question —
which PPU dot a fixed master-clock offset falls in depends on the CPU/PPU
alignment of the instruction, and that is exactly why the three writes measured
earlier cannot be satisfied by one dot-quantised constant. The "phase-exact
commit" hypothesis is not an alternative to the phi2 story; it IS the phi2
story, told at the resolution the evidence actually supports.

## THE DERIVATION, RUN — and it closes the line at 143/144

`rendering_enabled_delayed` was the constant worth deriving because it gates
BOTH remaining failures: the OAM2 63/255/339 reset (`Frozen OAM2 Increment`) and
the sprite-counter re-arm (`Stale Sprite Shift Regs` test 5). v2.6.17 swept it
1..4 and found 1 optimal, but only at the SHIPPED commit point, which answers a
different question. `render_gate_lag_derivation.rs` sweeps it against phi2.

| write | lag | passed | Frozen OAM2 | Stale | Arbitrary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **1** (shipped) | **143/144** | FAIL | **pass** | **pass** |
| 0 | 0 | 142/144 | FAIL | FAIL | **pass** |
| 0 | 2 | 141/144 | FAIL | FAIL | **pass** |
| **2** | **0** | 142/144 | **FAIL** | **pass** | FAIL |
| 2 | 1 | 141/144 | **pass** | FAIL | FAIL |
| 2 | 2 | 140/144 | FAIL | FAIL | FAIL |
| 4 | 0 | 142/144 | FAIL | **pass** | FAIL |
| 4 | 1 | 141/144 | **pass** | FAIL | FAIL |
| 4 | 2 | 140/144 | FAIL | FAIL | FAIL |

The grid is all nine cells of `write` in {0, 2, 4} x `lag` in {0, 1, 2}. A claim
that something is unreachable ACROSS a space has to have measured the space, and
the first run of this sweep had probed seven of the nine.

### CORRECTION: the first `lag = 2` numbers were the instrument, not the console

**Published as `120/144` for both `lag = 2` cells; they are 141 and 140.** The
depth-2 pipeline did not shift. `tick` re-pointed `rendering_enabled_delayed` to
`render_gate_prev2` at the top of the dot and then, at the bottom, assigned
`render_gate_prev2` FROM `rendering_enabled_delayed` — reading back the field the
re-point had already overwritten, so the assignment was `prev2 = prev2` and the
stage froze at its power-on `false` for the entire run. Every `lag >= 2` cell
therefore measured **a permanently disabled rendering gate**, which is exactly
what a 23-test collapse looks like, and that collapse read as evidence that
deeper pipelines are catastrophic when it was evidence of nothing.

Two things were wrong with the same knob, and only the first is the reviewer's:

1. **The general path froze.** Fixed by capturing stage N-1 before the re-point
   and shifting THAT into N-2. The two halves are now a named pair,
   `render_gate_begin_dot` / `render_gate_end_dot`, so the value that must
   travel between them is a return value rather than a field two hundred lines
   apart, and `render_gate_lag_shifts_a_two_dot_pipeline` fails on the pre-fix
   form at the first `true`.
2. **The specialized dot paths bypassed the pipeline entirely** — and worse than
   bypassing it, their guards prove only a ONE-dot rendering history
   (`rendering_enabled_delayed`, `prev_rendering_enabled`), which is what depths
   0 and 1 read. At depth >= 2 the gate reads rendering from two dots ago, about
   which the guard says nothing, so a dot with rendering enabled for the last two
   dots but disabled before them would run the rendering-enabled fast body while
   the general path gated it off. `fast_dot_paths_valid()` excludes them at depth
   >= 2; in the default build it is a `const fn` returning `true`, asserted at
   compile time so "it folds away" is a property of the constant rather than a
   claim about a run.

Found in review on #506 (Antigravity), against a sweep that had already been run
and written up. It is the project's own recurring shape — a disagreement with the
console that was a defect in the instrument reading it — and the reason it did
not survive is that the sweep asserts its control FIRST: `(0,1)` reads 143/144 in
both the broken and the fixed run, so the harness was never in question and the
defect had to be in what the depth knob did.

### The hypothesis is REFUTED

phi2 with one less delay — `write=2, lag=0` — was predicted to reproduce the
shipped effect time and close everything. It reads **142/144 with `Frozen OAM2
Increment` STILL FAILING**, and loses `Arbitrary Sprite zero`. The correction
that worked for `mask_for_skip_check` (2 -> 1) does not transfer.

### The stronger result: NO cell satisfies all three

Read the three columns rather than the counts, which is the discipline this
whole line keeps having to relearn:

- `Frozen OAM2 Increment` passes at **two** settings, `(2, 1)` and `(4, 1)` —
  which are the same verdict set, so the corpus cannot separate a write at 2.0
  dots from one at 2.5. Both cost three entries.
- `Stale Sprite Shift Regs` passes at `(0,1)`, `(2,0)` and `(4,0)`.
- `Arbitrary Sprite zero` passes at `(0,1)`, `(0,0)` and `(0,2)` — i.e. only at
  the shipped commit point, at ANY depth.

The intersection is **empty**. `(0,1)` — what ships — is the only cell taking two
of the three, and it is the maximum on this axis. The commit offset and the gate
depth trade `Stale` against `Arbitrary` directly, and `Frozen OAM2` needs a
setting that loses both.

The corrected `lag = 2` cells sharpen this rather than softening it. Depth 2 is
not catastrophic, as the broken numbers implied — it costs one or two entries —
but it never GAINS one at any commit offset, so the depth axis contributes
nothing in either direction and the trade is entirely the commit offset's.

**So 144/144 is NOT reachable by tuning the commit offset and the rendering-gate
depth, in any combination.** That is the refutation the derivation was written to
be capable of producing, and it is why both directions and both controls were
swept rather than only the cell the hypothesis liked.

### What this does and does not close

CLOSES: the "re-derive the constants against phi2" programme, on this axis. Four
attempts by patching, one by measurement, and now one by systematic sweep with
controls in both directions.

DOES NOT close: the possibility that the model needs SUB-DOT resolution rather
than better constants. Every cell above is a whole-dot setting, and this
project's own comment on `MASK_WRITE_DELAY` — sourced from TriCNES — says the
quantity is "2-3 dots ... sub-dot alignment dependent". A variable cannot be
fitted by a constant, and an empty intersection across every constant is exactly
what that looks like from the outside.

That is a structural change to how a `$2001` write's effect time is represented,
not a constant edit, and it should not be attempted to gain one test.

### Recommendation

**Close the line at 143/144 and keep the apparatus.** `Frozen OAM2 Increment`
stays in `KNOWN_FAILING` with its cause named and its cost measured. The knobs
(`WRITE_PHI_OFFSET`, `READ_PHI_OFFSET`, `RENDER_GATE_LAG`, `MASK_WRITE_DELAY`)
are all default-off or default-shipped and re-measure in minutes, so a future
attempt starts from evidence rather than rebuilding the instrument.

## CONDITION 2 SETTLED: the access placement is DOT-QUANTISED, and the
## constraints on it are provably contradictory

The reopening conditions asked for "a decision on reads, which this plan
explicitly did NOT settle". It is settled here, and settling it required
throwing away the unit the whole study had been using.

### The unit was wrong: master clocks, where only dots are observable

`Bus::run_ppu_to` advances the PPU with
`while self.ppu_clock + ppu_div <= target` -- **whole dots only**. A sub-dot
change to a split that does not cross a dot boundary therefore cannot be
observed by anything, and the sweep's own output had been saying so for two
releases without anyone reading it that way:

| pair | result |
|---|---|
| `READ=0` vs `READ=2` | identical counts AND identical gained/lost sets |
| `WRITE=2` vs `WRITE=4` | identical counts AND identical gained/lost sets |

Effective advance is `pre - PPU_OFFSET`: a read is `4 + r` master clocks and a
write `6 + w`, at 4 mc per dot. So `r` of 0 or 2 is dot 1 either way, `w` of 2
or 4 is dot 2 either way, and **the shipped read and the shipped write are in
the SAME dot** -- the celebrated "half a dot" between them is invisible by
construction.

This is physical rather than an artifact of the model. The 2C02 is clocked at
the dot rate, so an access lands in whichever dot its strobe falls in; there is
no sub-dot state for it to land between. An NTSC CPU cycle is three dots, so the
entire placement question is **which of three dots**, for each of two access
kinds: nine cells, of which three had ever been measured.

### The nine-cell grid

`access_dot_derivation.rs`, control-first at the shipped placement:

| read | write | passed | Frozen OAM2 | Stale | Arbitrary | Misaligned OAM2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dot0 | dot0 | 142 | FAIL | pass | pass | pass |
| dot0 | dot1 | 141 | **pass** | FAIL | pass | FAIL |
| dot0 | dot2 | 140 | FAIL | FAIL | FAIL | pass |
| dot1 | dot0 | 141 | FAIL | FAIL | pass | FAIL |
| **dot1** | **dot1** | **143** | FAIL | pass | pass | pass |
| dot1 | dot2 | 141 | **pass** | FAIL | FAIL | FAIL |
| dot2 | dot0 | 133 | FAIL | FAIL | pass | pass |
| dot2 | dot1 | 135 | FAIL | FAIL | pass | FAIL |
| dot2 | dot2 | 137 | FAIL | **pass** | **pass** | **pass** |

### Each entry turns out to constrain a different thing, and two of them fight

Reading the columns rather than the counts -- the discipline this line keeps
having to relearn -- every one of these is a clean rule, not a smear:

- **The six NMI entries** fail at `read = dot2` and nowhere else. They constrain
  the READ dot alone and admit dot 0 or dot 1.
- **`Stale Sprite Shift Regs`** passes at exactly the three diagonal cells and
  fails at all six off-diagonal ones. It constrains **`read == write`** and says
  nothing about where.
- **`Arbitrary Sprite zero`** fails only when `write = dot2` while
  `read != dot2`.
- **`Misaligned OAM2 Address`** fails at exactly the four cells where
  `|write - read| == 1`.
- **`Frozen OAM2 Increment`** -- the one this whole line is trying to close --
  passes at exactly `(dot0, dot1)` and `(dot1, dot2)`: **`write == read + 1`**,
  and nowhere else.

**The last two are contradictory, and both are OAM2 entries.** `Frozen OAM2
Increment` requires a spacing of exactly `+1`; `Misaligned OAM2 Address`
requires a spacing that is not `+/-1`. No placement satisfies both, so no
placement reaches 144, and the maximum over the whole grid is the 143 that
already ships.

That is a stronger result than "the intersection is empty" from the earlier
gate-lag sweep, because it is not a search outcome. It is two rules read off
nine measurements, and they are inconsistent as rules.

### The unified-access reading, and why it does not rescue it

`(dot2, dot2)` is the physically honest placement -- both accesses strobing in
the same phase of the CPU cycle, which is what hardware does -- and it passes
**all three** of the entries the shipped model is suspected of compensating for:
`Stale Sprite Shift Regs`, `Arbitrary Sprite zero` and `Misaligned OAM2
Address`. It loses exactly the six NMI entries, and `Frozen OAM2 Increment`
still fails.

So the tempting story is that the six NMI entries are the compensation and a
one-dot CPU/PPU alignment correction would restore them. **Tested and refuted.**
`VBL_SET_DOT` moves the flag-set dot on scanline 241, and at `(dot2, dot2)`:

| VBL set dot | passed | NMI six passing |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 131 | **0 of 6** |
| 1 (ships, and what nesdev specifies) | 137 | **0 of 6** |
| 2 | 133 | **1 of 6** |

Not one of the six comes back, and both non-default values break four more
entries besides. The read placement is independently constrained, not an
alignment artifact.

### Reads cannot be split into a sample point and an effect point

That was the leading hypothesis, and the documentation refutes it without a
sweep. The nesdev `NMI` page gives ONE instant for a `$2002` read -- "Return
old status of `vblank_flag` in bit 7, **then** set `vblank_flag` to false" --
and states the race as "if [VBlank set] and [read PPUSTATUS] happen
**simultaneously**". Observation and side effect are the same event, so there is
no second knob to place.

`PPU frame timing` adds the alignment caveat directly: the documented behaviour
holds for "one particular set of alignments... where a read will see a change to
a flag if and only if it **starts at or after** the PPU tick where the flag
changes", and other alignments shift visibility by a tick. Our reads land in the
cycle's first-third dot, which is that model.

### What the ROM's own comments do and do not settle

`AccuracyCoin.asm` annotates its `$2001` writes with predicted effect dots.
`Stale Sprite Shift Registers` test 5: the store "begins on scanline 3, dot 325.
Accounting for the delay, rendering should be disabled around dot 334 or 335",
and test 6: begins "scanline 4, dot 248 ... disabled around dot 258 or 259".

Those are +9/+10 and +10/+11 dots from the instruction's first dot. `STA abs` is
four cycles, so its write cycle spans +9..+11 -- the two predictions together
span the WHOLE write cycle. They pin the write to the fourth cycle and, taken
honestly, **do not discriminate dot 1 from dot 2 within it**. Recorded because
the opposite reading is easy to reach from either comment alone, and this plan
has already published one over-fine reading of a dot measurement.

### The decision

**Reads stay where they are, and so do writes.** Not as a deferral this time:
the placement axis is nine cells wide, all nine are measured, the shipped cell
is the unique maximum, and the two OAM2 entries impose contradictory
requirements on it. Closing `Frozen OAM2 Increment` is not a placement problem,
and conditions 1 and 3 of the reopening list are therefore **not the next step**
-- they were both premised on adopting a placement the measurement now refuses.

What the grid does hand forward is a much sharper target than "phi2 is right":
two OAM2 entries disagree about the read/write dot spacing by exactly one dot,
which is a statement about the OAM2 counter's own gating rather than about the
CPU. That is where the remaining work is.

## Risks

1. **The corpus re-baseline can bless a regression.** The whole risk of this
   version. Mitigation: the first-difference control in A, and per-test naming
   in C. Never re-baseline on a diff nobody read.
2. **Two dots may not be one number.** The offset was measured on `$2001`
   writes; reads and other registers may not share it. Measure per access
   kind before assuming a single constant.
3. **It may be refuted.** Named as an acceptable outcome in D so a null result
   is reportable rather than something to push past.
4. **The sibling co-simulation suite compares against this oracle.** A change
   in oracle behaviour re-opens those gates; budget for re-exporting goldens
   from a pinned commit.

## Out of scope

- **Rung 6 / hardware and the v2.7.0 submission.** Unchanged, still awaiting a
  board.
- **Any further OAM2 work.** `Frozen OAM2 Increment` stays pinned in
  `KNOWN_FAILING`. The version has now decided the alignment question — see
  *CLOSED* above — and the answer is that the compensation stays, so the entry
  stays with it until the three conditions listed there are met.
- **Re-litigating the v2.6.17 save-state epoch.** `PPU_SNAPSHOT_VERSION` 9 is
  an approved maintainer decision.
