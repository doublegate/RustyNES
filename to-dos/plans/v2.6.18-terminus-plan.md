# v2.6.18 "Terminus" — a CPU write lands where the cycle ENDS, and this core applies it where the cycle begins

> Covers exactly one release. Opened by a measurement taken during v2.6.17,
> recorded in that release's CHANGELOG under *Measured and rejected*.

## What opens the version: two dots, measured twice, on a ROM that names the dots

AccuracyCoin `Frozen OAM2 Increment` states the dots it writes on, which makes
the alignment directly checkable rather than inferable. Probing both `$2001`
writes the test performs, armed to the single frame the behaviour occurs in:

| write | the ROM names | this core applies |
|---|---|---|
| disable `$00` | scanline 194, dot 242 | scanline 194, dot **240** |
| enable `$1E` | dot 256 | dot **254** |

Both exactly **two dots early**, on two independent writes in one run.

The mechanism is not in dispute and is not a tuning constant. `Cpu::start_cycle`
catches the PPU up **before** the bus access, so a PPU register write is
applied at **M2-low** — the first of the CPU cycle's three PPU dots. A 6502
commits a write at **phi2**, the third. Everything downstream of a `$2001`,
`$2000`, `$2005`, `$2006` or `$4014` write therefore observes it two dots
earlier than hardware does.

**This is v2.5.4's finding one layer out.** That release found the
co-simulation TESTBENCH presenting every CPU access on the second of a cycle's
three dots and moved it to phi2; the EMULATOR applies them on the first, and
nothing has re-measured that since the v2.0.0 scheduler rewrite.

## Why this is a version and not a patch

The tempting fix was tried in v2.6.17 and is **refuted, with numbers**. Delaying
the rendering-enable gate (`rendering_enabled_delayed`, shipped lag 1) was swept
1..4 against the full battery with the mask-timing-sensitive tests as controls:

| lag | passed | Frozen OAM2 | Stale Sprite Shift Regs | Misaligned OAM2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 (shipped)** | **143/144** | FAIL | pass | pass |
| 2 | 141/144 | FAIL | FAIL | FAIL |
| 3 | 141/144 | FAIL | FAIL | FAIL |
| 4 | 140/144 | FAIL | FAIL | FAIL |

No value closes the test and every value above the shipped one regresses two
tests that pass today. **The gate is not the lever; the write placement is.**

Moving it shifts every PPU register write in every game. That reaches the
60-ROM commercial oracle, the visual baseline corpus, every committed golden
whose run contains a PPU register write, and the co-simulation suite in the
sibling. It is ADR 0029 territory and needs its own re-baselining, which is
precisely why it is scoped here instead of appended to v2.6.17.

## Deliverable

- **A. Establish the control BEFORE changing anything.** Capture the current
  per-cycle bus stream and the framebuffer/audio hashes for the corpus that
  will be re-baselined. The first difference after the change must be a PPU
  register write's effect dot and nothing else; anything earlier means the
  experiment changed rather than the write placement.

- **B. Move the write to phi2**, behind a default-off feature so the shipped
  build is byte-identical until the evidence says otherwise. The change is in
  the `start_cycle` / `end_cycle` ordering in `rustynes-core`, not in the PPU.

- **C. Measure, per test, with controls named.** AccuracyCoin by test name (not
  the headline count — v2.6.17 showed the count hides which tests moved),
  nestest 0-diff, blargg batteries, `cpu_interrupts_v2`, the commercial oracle,
  the visual corpus.

- **D. Decide on the evidence, and accept a refutation.** If phi2 placement is
  a net regression, that is a result: revert, keep the numbers, and record why
  the M2-low placement is load-bearing. This project has shipped rejected
  changes with their measurements before and this plan authorises it again.

- **E. If it lands: the ADR.** ADR 0029 states the scheduler's access model;
  moving the commit point amends it, and the amendment names what was
  re-baselined and why.

## The gate

- **`Frozen OAM2 Increment` is NOT the acceptance criterion.** It is one
  observation. Closing it while regressing anything else is a failure, and the
  v2.6.17 sweep is the worked example of why a single-test target misleads.
- Per-test AccuracyCoin comparison before and after, **by name**. Net
  improvement with zero named regressions, or the change does not land.
- nestest 0-diff, blargg 11/11, `cpu_interrupts_v2` 5/5 — all VERIFIED.
- Every re-baselined golden accompanied by the reason its bytes moved. A
  re-baseline with no stated cause is how a real regression gets blessed.
- The default build byte-identical while the feature is off, measured.

## Progress: the diagnosis is CONFIRMED, and the cost is three tests

Deliverable B is built (`phi2-write-sweep`, default off) and C's first sweep is
done. `write_split`'s `pre` is the lever, and the result is unambiguous:

| offset | commit lands at | passed | gained | lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 (shipped)** | 1.5 dots in | **143/144** | — | — |
| 1 | 1.75 dots in | 143/144 | — | — |
| **2** | **2.0 dots = phi2** | 141/144 | **Frozen OAM2 Increment** | Arbitrary Sprite zero, Stale Sprite Shift Regs, Misaligned OAM2 Address |
| 3 | past phi2 | 141/144 | Frozen OAM2 Increment | the same three |
| 4 | past phi2 | 141/144 | Frozen OAM2 Increment | the same three |

Two things fall out, and the second is the version's real subject.

**The root cause is confirmed.** Offset 2 is exactly where the commit crosses
into the cycle's third dot — phi2 — and that is the offset at which
`Frozen OAM2 Increment` starts passing. Offset 1 changes nothing at all,
because it does not cross the dot boundary. So the test is not sensitive to
"a bit later"; it is sensitive to *which dot the write commits on*, which is
the hypothesis, stated in advance, behaving as predicted.

**Three tests currently depend on the WRONG placement.** `Arbitrary Sprite
zero`, `Stale Sprite Shift Regs` and `Misaligned OAM2 Address` pass today and
fail at phi2. That is the v2.5.7 shape exactly — a wrong phase with windows
downstream compensating for it — and it means the version cannot be "move the
write and re-baseline". Each of the three has to be examined on its own terms
to establish whether it is a compensation that must move with the phase, or a
genuine behaviour the phi2 model gets wrong.

Note `Misaligned OAM2 Address` among them: v2.6.17 closed it by modelling
OAM2Address as a live counter, and that model was calibrated against the
M2-low write placement. It is the clearest candidate for a compensation.

**Nothing is adopted.** The shipped default stays offset 0 and the build is
byte-identical with the feature off (the `const` path is retained under
`cfg(not(feature))`, so the hot `write_split` pays no atomic load). 141 < 143,
so adopting now would be a net regression blessed by a single-test target —
precisely what this plan's gate forbids.

## Is `Misaligned OAM2 Address` a compensation? YES — and it is a SUB-DOT one

Asked directly, and answered by measurement rather than by argument. The ROM
stores the byte it read from `$2004` at zero page `$50`, and that test is the
LAST catalog entry, so the byte survives a full battery run and can be read
with no instrumentation at all:

| configuration | `$0050` | OAM2Address reached |
|---|---|---|
| shipped | `$06` | **`$18`** — the value the ROM expects |
| write +2 | `$A3` | **`$17`** — exactly one short |
| read +2, write +2 | `$A3` | `$17` |

`$A3` is OAM2 index `$17`; `$06` is index `$18`. Moving the write costs
**exactly one increment**.

**Two hypotheses were killed on the way to the answer.**

*It is not read/write spacing.* The first sweep moved writes alone, which
changes the gap between a write and a following read rather than moving the
access model as a unit — a real flaw in that experiment. But at read +2 /
write +2, where the spacing is preserved, the test still fails with the same
`$A3`. The failure tracks the WRITE offset alone.

*It is not the dot arithmetic.* Running the implementation's own rule —
increment on even dots in 256..=320 while rendering is enabled — over each
placement gives **24 increments (`$18`) in all three cases**, including the
dots the ROM itself names. The window is 18 dots wide with 9 even dots inside
it either way. The counter's dot model is placement-INDEPENDENT and correct.

**So what is calibrated is the sub-dot boundary.** `tick_oam_bus` is called
once per dot, gated on `mask.rendering_enabled()`. A `$2001` write mutates
that mask partway THROUGH a dot, so whether the boundary dot counts depends on
whether the write landed before or after that dot's tick. At the shipped
M2-low placement the boundary falls the right way; move the commit two dots
and it flips, costing one increment.

**What this means for the version, and it is good news.** The v2.6.17
OAM2Address counter does not need redesigning — its arithmetic is already
independent of where the write lands. What needs deriving is the ordering
between a mask write and the per-dot OAM2 tick within a single dot. That is a
much smaller and better-defined job than "recalibrate the counter", and it is
the same class of question v2.5.4 answered for the co-simulation testbench.

## Reads must NOT simply move to phi2

Measured while answering the above, and it changes the plan's shape. Moving
the READ sample point to dot 2.0 (offset +4) does not merely fail to help — it
costs **six NMI tests** (`NMI Overlap BRK`, `NMI Overlap IRQ`, `NMI Timing`,
`NMI Suppression`, `NMI at VBlank end`, `NMI disabled at VBlank`), drops the
battery to **137/144**, and UNDOES the `Frozen OAM2 Increment` gain that the
write move buys.

The read sample point is doing real work for NMI and VBlank timing. "A 6502
samples reads at phi2 too, so move both" is the naive reading and the numbers
refuse it. Whatever the final model is, it is not one offset applied to both
halves of the access.

## The sub-dot ordering: located exactly, and one fix attempt refuted

The ordering question is now answered precisely. Instrumenting which dots the
OAM2 maintenance actually sees rendering enabled on, for the misaligned
scanline:

| placement | disabled dots | even dots inside | counter ends at |
|---|---|---|---|
| shipped (writes land 266 / 284) | **[267 … 285]** | 9 | `$18` — correct |
| write +2 (writes land 268 / 286) | **[268 … 286]** | **10** | `$17` — one short |

Both windows are **19 dots wide**. What differs is their PARITY: the first
starts and ends on odd dots, the second on even ones, so the second contains
one extra even dot and the counter loses exactly one increment. The ROM's own
window — disable ON 268, enable ON 286 — is 18 dots, [268..=285], with 9 even
dots, which is the correct `$18`.

So the defect is not the counter and not the window length. **The write's
effect lands one dot late on the trailing edge**, and how late depends on
where the write's master clock falls inside a dot, which is exactly what the
commit-point move changes.

### Attempt 1: quantise the gate to a dot boundary — REFUTED

Latched the `$2001` value at write time together with `self.dot`, and applied
it from the next dot boundary, so the OAM2 maintenance would stop reading the
live mask and the window's parity would follow the dot the write landed on
rather than sub-dot rounding. Predicted `$18` at both placements.

Measured: `$06` at the shipped placement (unchanged) and **`$A3` at write +2**
— no change at all. The latch does not decouple them, because `self.dot` at
write time is itself a function of how far `run_ppu_to` has advanced, so the
quantisation inherits the same rounding it was meant to remove. Reverted; the
prediction and its refutation are the result.

**This is the fourth attempt on this behaviour** (freeze `base = 0`,
rendering-enable lag, write commit offset, gate quantisation), which is this
project's own threshold for questioning the approach instead of trying a
fifth. The next move is not another patch at the OAM2 gate: it is to decide
what the dot grid means for a write at all — i.e. whether `run_ppu_to` should
advance to a dot BOUNDARY before applying an access, so that "the dot a write
happened on" is well defined independently of the commit offset. That is a
scheduler question, which is the version this plan is for.

## Deliverable A is DONE — and the obvious instrument was rejected first

`crates/rustynes-test-harness/tests/terminus_control.rs` captures the
first-difference control on the SHIPPED scheduler, and is demonstrated by
mutation to catch the exact change this version will make.

**The co-simulation checkpoint chain was built first and thrown away.** It is
the obvious instrument — a rolling per-cycle FNV at 16 bytes per 4096 cycles,
with `first_full_capture_difference` to localise — and a corpus of four
workloads was exported and committed. Then the mutation pass: moving the write
commit two master clocks produced a **byte-identical chain**. Not a weak
signal; no signal at all.

The reason is structural and was worth the detour to find. Those chains need
`--irq-trace`, `irq-timing-trace` selects a different `for sub_dot in 0..3`
loop in `Bus::tick_one_cpu_cycle` (the v2.4.1 finding), and that loop does not
go through `write_split`. **The instrument is blind to the subject of this
version by construction** — a gate that would have reported "no change" while
the scheduler moved underneath it. Deleted rather than kept with a caveat.

The replacement runs the DEFAULT feature set and hashes what a user observes
(pre-palette framebuffer + work RAM, per frame, rolling FNV-1a).
`TERMINUS_DUMP=<dir>` writes the per-frame chain so the earliest differing
frame can be read off.

**Measured coverage, stated rather than implied.** Under the phi2 mutation:

| workload | moves? |
|---|---|
| `nestest` (400 frames) | **yes** — the control is not blind |
| `ppu_vbl_nmi/01-vbl_basics` | no, at 60 frames or 400 |
| `sprite_hit_tests/01.basics` | no, at 60 frames or 400 |

The second half is a finding, not a gap. Those two workloads are measurably
INSENSITIVE to a two-master-clock shift in the write commit, so the corpus
needing re-baseline when the dot grid moves is narrower than "everything with
a PPU write in it". Knowing which workloads do NOT move is what keeps a
re-baseline from being blessed wholesale.

The depth is also a measurement: at 60 frames only `nestest` moved because the
blargg ROMs were still initialising, which is the "stimulus blind" outcome in
this project's taxonomy. 400 frames is the remediation; `AccuracyCoin` was
dropped rather than lengthened, since reaching its battery needs a START press
and thousands of frames that `accuracycoin.rs` already drives.

So A is satisfied and the dot grid can move: any change now has a baseline to
be judged against, on the scheduler that actually ships.

## B and C are done: the move was made, and it DOES NOT LAND

The write commit was moved to phi2 and diffed against the control. Deliverable
D's authorisation applies: this is a refutation kept with its numbers.

### The first difference is exactly where it should be

| after N frames (nestest) | framebuffer pixels differing | work RAM bytes differing |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 0 | 0 |
| 4 | 0 | 0 |
| **5** | **6 of 61,440** | **0 of 2,048** |

Six pixels, no RAM, nothing earlier. A sub-tile sliver from a PPU register
write landing two dots later — the change is the subject, not something
upstream. The other two control workloads are byte-identical across all 400
frames. On the control's own terms the experiment is clean.

### And the cost, measured with `--no-fail-fast`

The first attempt at this measurement was WRONG and is worth recording: plain
`cargo test` stops at the first failing binary, so `accuracycoin` failed and
nestest, blargg and everything after it never ran. It looked like "only
AccuracyCoin moves". Re-running with `--no-fail-fast` gives the real picture —
**83 binaries ok, 4 failed, 10 failing tests**:

| what | detail |
|---|---|
| AccuracyCoin | **141/144**, down from 143. Gains `Frozen OAM2 Increment`; loses `Misaligned OAM2 Address`, `Stale Sprite Shift Regs`, `Arbitrary Sprite zero` |
| **blargg `ppu_vbl_nmi` 10-even_odd_timing** | **FAILS with code 09**, was passing |
| visual baselines | 6 framebuffer goldens move (`flowing_palette` x3, `full_palette` x2, `scanline`, `nmi_sync_demo`) |

### The decision, against the gate this plan wrote in advance

The gate says: *net improvement with zero named regressions, or the change
does not land.* Net is **-2 on AccuracyCoin plus an independent-oracle
regression**, so **it does not land**.

The distinction that matters for whoever picks this up: the six framebuffer
goldens are BASELINES — if phi2 is right they legitimately move and get
re-blessed. `ppu_vbl_nmi/10-even_odd_timing` is not a baseline. It is a
third-party ROM with its own pass/fail verdict, and it went from pass to
`09`. That is evidence against naive phi2 placement, not a golden awaiting a
re-bless, and it is the single most important number here.

So the position is: phi2 is the right DIAGNOSIS (it closes the test the whole
line started from, and the first difference is clean) and the wrong CHANGE as
applied — moving the write alone, with reads left at dot 1.0 and the three
dependent behaviours un-rederived. At least one of those three is proven to be
a sub-dot compensation. The even-odd frame timing regression suggests a
fourth interaction nobody has looked at yet.

Next, and not before: re-derive `10-even_odd_timing` under phi2 from the ROM's
own statement of what it measures, since that is an independent oracle and
therefore adjudicates. The three AccuracyCoin compensations come after.

## `10-even_odd_timing` re-derived under phi2 — the oracle regression CLOSES

The ROM states its own verdict, which is where this started rather than ended.
Under phi2 it reports:

> `Failed #2` — **"Clock is skipped too soon, relative to enabling BG"**

### The codebase already contained the compensation, and says so

`Ppu::mask_for_skip_check` is a two-stage delay pipeline consumed by nothing
except the pre-render dot-339 odd-frame skip. Its own doc comment names the
cause exactly:

> "enough to compensate for the lockstep bus model applying `cpu_write` at the
> *start* of a CPU cycle (before the cycle's 3 PPU ticks) while real hardware
> latches the write at φ2 (effectively the *end* of the cycle)."

So a previous author independently diagnosed this version's root cause and
worked around it LOCALLY, for one detector, years before it was named
globally. That is the strongest evidence yet that phi2 is the right model:
the workaround exists, it is documented, and it describes the same defect.

Under phi2 the compensation becomes a DOUBLE correction, which is precisely
what "too soon, relative to enabling BG" reports.

### The re-derivation is a HALVING, not a removal

Predicted: remove the compensation entirely. **Wrong, and measured wrong** —
at zero stages the ROM flips to `Failed #3` "skipped too **late**", at both
placements. The correct value is bracketed, and it is one stage:

| skip-check delay | shipped write | phi2 write |
|---|---|---|
| 2 stages (shipped) | **pass** `08 08 09 07` | `#2` too soon |
| 1 stage | `#3` too late | **pass** `08 08 09 07` |
| 0 stages | `#3` too late | `#3` too late |

At one stage under phi2 the ROM prints the SAME four values as the shipped
configuration does today. Not merely a pass — the same measurement.

### What that leaves

Re-running everything at phi2 + one stage: **85 binaries ok, 3 failed**
(against 83 / 4 for naive phi2).

* `ppu_vbl_nmi/10-even_odd_timing` — **no longer failing.** The only
  ADJUDICATING regression is gone.
* AccuracyCoin — still 141/144: gains `Frozen OAM2 Increment`, still loses
  `Misaligned OAM2 Address`, `Stale Sprite Shift Regs`, `Arbitrary Sprite
  zero`. Those three are the remaining compensations.
* Six framebuffer baselines still move, which is bookkeeping if phi2 is right.

The gate still is not met — net AccuracyCoin is -2 — so it still does not
land. But the blocker that mattered is removed: what remains are three
compensations of the kind already proven to exist, not an independent oracle
saying the model is wrong.

## `Arbitrary Sprite zero` and `Stale Sprite Shift Regs` — read, and NOT closed

Both were read at the source and one hypothesis was tested and refuted. What
they need is recorded; what they are not is guesswork.

### What each actually asserts

`Stale Sprite Shift Regs` fails at **test 5**, which the ROM titles *"What if
we prepare the sprite counter but don't let the counter activate on dot 339?"*
and explains in full:

> "If the ppu is rendering on dot 339, then the shifter counters are set to
> 'counting'. If rendering was not enabled on dot 339, the shifter counters
> will be in whatever state they were previously in, which is likely
> 'halted'."

It is a second **dot-339 rendering gate**, alongside the odd-frame skip.
The ROM even states the write delay it assumes: `STA $2001` "begins on
scanline 3, dot 325. Accounting for the delay, rendering should be disabled
around dot 334 or 335" — i.e. the commit lands in the instruction's LAST
cycle, roughly nine dots after it starts. That is a direct, independent
statement of the phi2 model from the test author.

`Arbitrary Sprite zero` fails at **test 3**, *"Misaligned OAM can properly
draw a sprite, and yes, it can even trigger a sprite zero hit"* — sprite
evaluation with `OAMADDR` not a multiple of four. Its code is shared with
`Misaligned OAM behavior` and `$2004 behavior`, so the three move together.

### The hypothesis that was tried, and refuted

At dot 339 this core has TWO rendering gates with DIFFERENT delays: the
sprite-counter re-arm reads `rendering_enabled_delayed` (one stage), the
odd-frame skip reads `mask_for_skip_check` (two stages). Since the skip needed
2 -> 1 under phi2, the natural hypothesis was that every gate loses one stage,
so the counter re-arm should go 1 -> 0.

Implemented (moving the re-arm out of the `rendering_gate` block onto the live
mask) and measured: **all three still fail, unchanged**. And the reason it was
inert is visible in the ROM's own setup — test 5 holds rendering DISABLED
across dot 339, so a gate on the live mask skips the block exactly as the
one-stage gate did. The knob was never on the path the test exercises.

### Where this stops, and why

That is a third refuted attempt in this family (gate quantisation, one-stage
-less, and before them the rendering-enable lag). The threshold for
questioning the approach rather than reaching for a fourth guess has been
passed again, and the honest next step is a PROBE rather than a patch:
instrument the dot at which rendering actually transitions for test 5's two
`$2001` writes, at both placements, the way the OAM2 window was localised.
That measurement is cheap, it is what settled the OAM2 question, and it will
say whether the disable is landing before or after 339 in each configuration
instead of leaving it to be inferred.

Nothing was adopted. The tree is unchanged and AccuracyCoin holds 143/144.

## The probe, the README, and TWO ERRORS THAT WERE CANCELLING

The probe settled the write-placement question, the upstream README supplied a
behaviour this core was missing, and together they change the verdict on phi2.

### The probe: both placements are INSIDE the ROM's stated window

Logged every `$2001` write on the isolating sub-test ROM
(`ppu-misc-stale-sprite-shift-regs.nes`), which removes the battery's other
traffic entirely. Test 5's pair:

| placement | disable lands | enable lands |
|---|---|---|
| shipped | scanline 3, dot **334** | scanline 4, dot **161** |
| phi2 | scanline 3, dot **335** | scanline 4, dot **162** |

The ROM states its own expectation: the disable "should be disabled around dot
334 or 335", the enable "around dot 161 or 162". **Both placements land inside
both windows.** So the write placement is not what decides this test, and the
"two dots early" framing from the earlier Frozen OAM2 probe does not
generalise — +2 master clocks is half a dot, which crosses a dot boundary
only sometimes.

Also measured and refuted: an alignment fragility. Across **eight power-on
seeds** the verdict byte at `$048F` is `01` (pass) for every seed at the
shipped placement and `16` (fail, code 5) for every seed at phi2. Deterministic
on the placement, not on alignment.

### The README: an increment this core never performed

AccuracyCoin's README documents `Frozen OAM2 Increment` test 3 as:

> "If the OAM2 address overflows for any reason, including the end of sprite
> fetch where **the address is incremented on dot 321**"

This core increments `OAM2Address` on dots 256..=320 only. Adding dot 321 —
subject to the freeze flag, as the same sentence implies — and the arithmetic
closes exactly against the dot-window measurement taken earlier:

| placement | increments 256..320 | + dot 321 | reads | expected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| shipped | 24 | **25 = `$19`** | `$C1` | `$06` |
| phi2 | 23 | **24 = `$18`** | `$06` | `$06` |

**So `Misaligned OAM2 Address` was passing on the shipped build because two
errors cancelled**: a missing dot-321 increment (one too few) against a write
landing a dot early (one extra even dot inside the disabled window). Each is
wrong; together they produced the right byte. That is this project's
"two traces agreeing was two errors cancelling" pattern, and only a documented
third fact could expose it.

### Where the combination stands

phi2 + dot-321 increment + the odd-frame skip at one stage:

| configuration | AccuracyCoin | failing binaries |
|---|---|---|
| baseline (shipped) | **143/144** | — |
| naive phi2 | 141/144 | 4, incl. the blargg oracle |
| + skip re-derived | 141/144 | 3 |
| **+ dot-321 increment** | **142/144** | 3 |

Still one short of the baseline, so it still does not land — but the two
regressions that remain are `Stale Sprite Shift Regs` and `Arbitrary Sprite
zero`, and `Misaligned OAM2 Address` has moved from a compensation to a
correctly-derived pass.

### The next lead, from the same README

`Arbitrary Sprite zero` test 3 is "Misaligned OAM should be able to trigger a
sprite zero hit", and the README's adjacent `Misaligned OAM Behavior` entries
give the mechanics it rests on — including a rule this core should be checked
against:

> "Misaligned OAM should stay misaligned until an object's Y position is out
> of the range of this scanline, at which point the OAM address is incremented
> by 4 and bitwise ANDed with `$FC`" — and, if secondary OAM is full at that
> moment, "you should instead only increment the OAM address by 5."

That is the same shape as the dot-321 finding: a specific, documented
behaviour to check the implementation against rather than a timing knob to
sweep. Do that before touching any gate.

### Research note

The nesdev wiki does NOT document the sprite shifter counter's
halted/counting state at dot 339 at all; `PPU rendering` and `PPU sprite
evaluation` cover the shift-register reloads and the odd-frame skip and stop
there. For this behaviour AccuracyCoin's own prose IS the specification, which
is why rung 2 of the provenance ladder was exhausted without result here.

## Risks

1. **The corpus re-baseline can bless a regression.** The whole risk of this
   version. Mitigation: the first-difference control in A, and per-test naming
   in C. Never re-baseline on a diff nobody read.
2. **Two dots may not be one number.** The offset was measured on `$2001`
   writes; reads and other registers may not share it. Measure per access
   kind before assuming a single constant.
3. **It may be refuted.** Named as an acceptable outcome in D so a null result
   is reportable rather than something to push past.
4. **The sibling co-simulation suite compares against this oracle.** A change
   in oracle behaviour re-opens those gates; budget for re-exporting goldens
   from a pinned commit.

## Out of scope

- **Rung 6 / hardware and the v2.7.0 submission.** Unchanged, still awaiting a
  board.
- **Any further OAM2 work.** `Frozen OAM2 Increment` stays pinned in
  `KNOWN_FAILING` until this version decides the alignment question.
- **Re-litigating the v2.6.17 save-state epoch.** `PPU_SNAPSHOT_VERSION` 9 is
  an approved maintainer decision.
