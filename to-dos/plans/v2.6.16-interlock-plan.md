# v2.6.16 "Interlock" — every gate passes and the deadline is still missed, and both are true

## What opens the version: a number that decides what ships, and it is wrong

`rtl/emu.sv` carries the argument for keeping the cartridge on the die. Two of
its sentences are refuted by measurement on a tree that is functionally
identical to the one they describe:

> Configured `USE_SDRAM_CART` ... **passes 140 of 142 co-simulation gates.**
> ... **The two CNROM gates fail on exactly that**, through the harness's own
> PRG check.

Run off-die at v2.6.15, the console passes **147 of 147, 0 failed, 0 skipped** —
including both CNROM gates, at 44 and 146 identical checkpoints. The
configuration was verified rather than assumed: the stamp file
`obj_dir_cpu/.use_sdram_1` is present, the rebuilt binary carries 25 SDRAM
symbols, and `USE_SDRAM` reaching Verilator as `-G` rather than as a file is the
exact defect v2.6.13 closed with that stamp.

Nothing in the SDRAM path has changed since v2.6.13 except comments;
`tb/cpu_main.cpp` and `tb/cpu6502_cosim.sv` are untouched; the only harness edit
is an additive make target. So the number was almost certainly measured
mid-release, before v2.6.13 restored the anti-contention deferral, and nothing
re-measured it — **the defect class v2.6.15 was named for, sitting in the file
that decides which bitstream ships.**

## And the deadline is still missed, which is the other half

The instrument built to measure it says so, unchanged:

```text
ok   worst CHR latency 15 cycles, budget 22
     worst PRG latency 28 cycles, the CPU samples at 24
ok   and short of the CPU's deadline by 4, as expected
```

**Both statements are true at once.** Every console gate passes, and the
deadline the design is known to miss is still missed, because the console's
stimulus never reaches the arbiter's synthetic worst case. That is *agreement
about an unasked question*, and this project has been bitten by that shape
before — a new module's gate that passed first try and caught nothing.

So 147 of 147 is **not** a licence to flip `USE_SDRAM_CART`. It is evidence
about coverage, not about the design. Recording it as if it were the second
would be the same error in the opposite direction from the one being corrected.

## Deliverable

- **A. Correct the refuted measurement** (`rtl/emu.sv`, `docs/sdram.md`,
  `docs/rung7-mappers.md`), stating **both** halves. Collapsing them either way
  is wrong: "the off-die build passes everything" invites the flip, and "two
  gates fail" is false.

- **B. The slot-scheduled arbiter — the subject.** The console's access pattern
  is fully deterministic, so each requester can hold a guaranteed slot instead
  of competing for one. The present arbiter is strict fixed-priority and grants
  one at a time; a PRG read arriving behind a CHR fetch pays that fetch's whole
  access first, which is where the four cycles go.

  **The goal is a PROOF, not a passing gate.** The gates already pass. What is
  missing is a guarantee that the worst case cannot exceed the budget, and that
  is what scheduling supplies and arbitration cannot.

- **C. The off-die flip** — **conditional and probably deferred again.** It
  needs B to close *and* a fallback for a missing SDRAM module, because an
  off-die core cannot run at all without the add-on while the on-die build needs
  none. "Needs no SDRAM" is the submission's differentiator, so the shipped
  default stays on-die regardless; the flip only ever produces a *variant*.

- **D. `docs/submission-case.md`** — written at the end of v2.6.15 and carried
  here unreviewed. It is the document v2.7.0's email links, so it should pass
  through a release with review rather than be appended to the submission.

- **E. The `.srf`**, with `tb/quartus-warnings.txt` regenerated in the same
  change. It was scheduled for "the next release that rebuilds the bitstream",
  and B forces one.

- **F. Research through the release**, per the standing instruction: re-verify
  `Template_MiSTer` HEAD (the `sys/` gate makes this a diff), and re-read the
  contributing page, which is actively edited.

## The gate

- The arbiter gate's own assertion **flips**: it currently asserts the shortfall
  (`short of the CPU's deadline by 4, as expected`). If B closes, that line must
  become a margin assertion, and the change in the gate is the evidence. If B
  does not close, the assertion stays and says so.
- Worst PRG latency **under 24 cycles** with stated margin, measured by
  `tb/sdram_arb_main.cpp` against synthetic worst-case traffic — not by the
  console gates, which have now been shown not to reach it.
- The suite stays **147 of 147 on the die**, which is what ships, and off-die.
- Every new or changed gate demonstrated to fail by mutation.
- Bitstream rebuilt, timing at all four corners, and **`BUILD_DATE` matching the
  filename datecode** — v2.6.15's own check.

## Outcome of B, recorded against the gate above rather than around it

**B does not close, and the plan's own instruction applies: the assertion stays
and says so.** `tb/sdram_arb_main.cpp` still reports worst PRG latency 28 against
24 under synthetic traffic. What changed is what that sentence is a statement
about, and it took three measurements.

1. **The shortfall is entirely contention.** With CHR silent the worst PRG
   latency is **15 against the same 24**, so the access has nine cycles of margin
   and a schedule is the right lever if one is needed.
2. **The console never produces the collision the gate constructs.** At the
   arbiter's own ports, off-die, across three workloads and fifteen million SDRAM
   cycles: **zero coincidences, minimum separation two cycles.** The CPU's worst
   latency there is **24 against 24** — met, always, on 240,303 requests.
3. **The fetch exceeds its derived budget and that budget is too tight.** Worst
   CHR is **23 against a derived 22**, on about eight requests in a hundred thousand —
   and the `CHR_LAT` sweep shows the console still matches the oracle on all
   61,440 pixels with **two more master clocks** of delay, failing only at three.
   So the real tolerance is at least eight and fewer than twelve cycles above the
   worst case, and the 22 understates it by at least nine.

**So the slot schedule is still worth building, and for a different reason than
the plan gave.** Not to fix a carried defect — there is none on the console — but
because the CPU currently meets its deadline at **zero margin**, so any future
change that adds a single cycle anywhere on that path breaks it. That is exactly
the shape v2.6.13 recorded when a control and the deployed code differed by one
cycle. `tb/sdram_latency_gate.py` now fails the moment it happens, which is the
part that could not wait for the redesign.

**What did close:** the deadline is measurable on the console instead of
inferred, and the four-copy address map that could point `$2007` at the wrong
region is gated.

## Risks

1. **B may not close.** Slot scheduling is a redesign of `sdram_arbiter.sv`, not
   a tuning of it. If it does not, the deliverable is the corrected measurement
   plus a written account of what was tried — a rejected design with its numbers
   is a result, and this project records those.
2. **The temptation to flip on 147 of 147.** Named here so it reads as a
   decision rather than an oversight. The console gates do not reach the worst
   case; that is the finding, not a clearance.
3. **A bitstream rebuild re-opens timing and the seed.** An RTL change to the
   arbiter invalidates the fit; expect to re-measure all four corners and
   possibly re-sweep the seed, and treat a re-seed as a prompt to look for
   utilisation nobody has measured rather than as maintenance.
4. **The `.srf` has a demonstrated failure mode** — a malformed one made Quartus
   abort after the resource summary with exit status 0. Adopt upstream's file
   and adapt the instance paths deliberately, not alongside other work.

## Out of scope

- **Rung 6 / hardware.** No board. Checked each release rather than assumed.
- **The submission email.** v2.7.0 by definition, and gated on hardware.
- **Mappers beyond the approved six**, FDS, expansion audio, savestates —
  v2.8+ by the approved plan.
- **Flipping `USE_SDRAM_CART` by default.** Even if B closes, on-die is the
  submission artifact.
