# v2.7.0 "Shakedown" — the board, the four properties, and a decision that is not a formality

A shakedown cruise is the first real voyage of a ship that has passed every dock
test. Its purpose is not to confirm the dock tests; it is to find what the dock
could not hold still long enough to measure. That is this release exactly: 152
co-simulation gates are green and **four properties sit downstream of every one
of them by construction**, and the only instrument that can reach them is a
board.

**Supersedes the rung-6 and rung-7 rows of**
[`v2.7.0-mister-core-plan.md`](v2.7.0-mister-core-plan.md), whose ladder is
otherwise delivered and whose own header says its version labels are stale.
Rung 7 (five mapper families) closed at v2.6.9 and the SDRAM controller at
v2.6.13, both **out of ladder order**, because rung 6 was blocked for nine
releases on hardware that did not exist here.

## What opens the version

A **SuperStation One is in hand**. That is the whole difference, and it is worth
stating what it does and does not change: every release from v2.6.6 onward has
carried the sentence *"no hardware has run this bitstream"*, and that sentence
is about to become false. Nothing else about the core changed to make it so.

v2.6.21 was the pre-flight and it did its job: battery saves exist, the deploy
loop the runbook prescribed exists, a guard that would have fired on **this
release's success** was replaced, and a coverage claim about the CHR-during-
rendering path was retracted and re-gated RED. Bring-up starts from a base whose
records are true, which was the entire point.

## The deliverable

Two things, and the second is gated on the first:

1. **Rung 6 closes on one board** — a `.rbf` boots a SuperStation One, runs
   ROMs, and the four properties no gate can reach are *measured*.
2. **The submission decision is made on evidence**, not assumed. v2.7.0 is the
   MiSTer-devel submission by the programme's own definition, and the honest
   form of that is a decision with a measurement behind it rather than an email
   scheduled in advance.

## The gate

| | |
|---|---|
| Rung 6 | every step in `docs/bringup.md` §"First boot" carries a **named artefact** — a capture, an FFT, a scope trace, a photograph — recorded against its verdict |
| Four properties | palette, video timing constants, audio absolute level, band-limiting: each **measured**, each with its number written down |
| Tier 1 | the on-hardware ROM battery, unattended, read back as **bytes** where the ROM permits it |
| Tier 2 | A/B against stock `NES_MiSTer` **on the same silicon** |
| Incumbent | re-measured on the **same** AccuracyCoin build before any submission decision |
| Checklist | `to-dos/mister/contribution-checklist.md` — every box that *can* be evidence about this core is ticked or carries a reason |

**"Runs on real hardware" is not a pass criterion and must not be used as one.**
A board that reaches the menu and shows a black screen satisfies it. That is why
the gate above is per-step artefacts rather than a single boolean.

## Strand A — platform, before the core is touched

`docs/HARDWARE_TESTING.md` §2 is already written and already accurate; this
strand is executing it, not designing it.

- **Reflash the SD card even on a new unit** (§2.1). Factory cards ship with
  overlapping partitions that corrupt silently. Installer v1.2, 2026-07-24.
- `update_all.sh`, replace the stock INIs and `yc.txt`, **record the four DIP
  switches** (§2.2–2.4).
- USB-C PD at **9 V/3 A**. A marginal supply mimics SDRAM corruption, which is
  the single most expensive misdiagnosis available on this platform.
- **§2.6's eight baseline smoke rows, with the stock `NES_MiSTer` core, not
  ours.** Do not proceed until they pass. A platform fault found after our core
  is loaded costs a day of looking in the wrong repository.

The corpus goes in **two** places, per v2.6.21's correction: `games/NES/tests/`
for the stock core's baseline and §5.3's A/B, and `games/RustyNES/tests/` for
our MGLs. Same bytes.

## Strand B — first boot, nine steps, stop at the first failure

`docs/bringup.md` orders these so that what a step *rules out* is worth more
than what it confirms. Copy `releases/RustyNES_20260919.rbf` to `_Console/`.

The shipped bitstream is the **on-die** build (`USE_SDRAM_CART = 1'b0`), so it
needs no SDRAM at all — the SS1's 128 MB is surplus here. The controller is
still instantiated and still runs its power-up and refresh, so a wiring or
phase defect can still show even though nothing reads it.

**Each step gets an artefact.** Step 7 ("a picture, and it is the right
picture") is the one to be most careful with: six commercial titles already
render byte-identically to the oracle over all 61,440 pixels **in simulation**,
so a wrong picture here is a palette or video-path question and *not* a
rendering one. The ladder has already compared what the PPU emits.

## Strand C — the four properties, which is why a board matters

| property | instrument | note |
|---|---|---|
| palette | colour-bar capture vs the FBX Smooth reference | the PPU gate compares the **pre-palette** index, deliberately |
| video timing constants | H/V on a scope | **a display that syncs is not proof the numbers are right** |
| audio absolute level | DC-couple, measure the resting level with silence | the `FRAC` 8→15 fix for a +127 offset was **never gated** |
| band-limiting | listen, and capture | absent by design; expect harsher treble. **A known difference, not a defect** |

These are the first four measurements after boot. Everything else a board can
tell you, 152 gates already have.

## Strand D — batteries, and a readback channel that only half exists

**This is the strand where the previous planning round was wrong, and the
correction changes the work.**

That round said: read the on-device AccuracyCoin result back through v2.6.21's
new save path and diff the vector entry-for-entry. **It cannot be done as
stated.** The save path persists the cartridge PRG-RAM window, `$6000-$7FFF`.
AccuracyCoin writes its results into **CPU RAM** — `$0457`, `$0458`, `$0459`,
`$0489`, `$048D`, `result_DrawTest = $03FF`, the whole vector inside
`$0300-$04FF`. None of it is in the save window, so none of it reaches `.sav`.

What the save path *does* buy is real and was the stated reason for building it:
**blargg's suites write their status to `$6000-$6003`**, which is exactly the
window. So:

- **Tier 1 blargg and `instr_test-v5` become a readback**, unattended, via
  `tools/run_battery.sh`. `HARDWARE_TESTING.md`'s automation note (§5.2, the
  paragraph beginning "Most blargg suites report results both on screen and via
  `$6000`") describes exactly this and is correct — it is just narrower than
  "everything reads back", and it says so itself: *"Where it cannot, fall back
  to capture-card frame grabs."* AccuracyCoin is the "cannot".
- **AccuracyCoin stays a screen**, exactly as `docs/bringup.md` already says,
  and that document's honesty is worth preserving rather than overwriting: *"a
  photograph is not 149 status bytes, and reading one is a human transcribing a
  picture. A clean screen is evidence the console runs; it is not evidence the
  vector still matches."*

**So there is an optional work item, and it is optional deliberately.** Patch
AccuracyCoin to mirror `$0300-$04FF` into `$6000+` and set the battery bit in
its header; the save path then carries all 149 bytes off the board and the
on-device comparison becomes the entry-for-entry diff rung 5 does in simulation.
It is the same family as v2.6.20's one-byte sub-test patch and it needs the same
control: **the patched ROM must produce a byte-identical vector to the unpatched
one in simulation** before a single hardware reading is taken from it. If that
control does not come out clean, drop the patch and keep the photograph — a
readback channel that alters the thing it reads is worse than no channel.

Also in this strand: **Tier 2 A/B against stock `NES_MiSTer` on the same
silicon**, and **Tier 4 soak**, 4+ hours, passive cooling being the harsher
test, including save/load across a power cycle — which is `T-MISTER-SAVE`'s
hardware acceptance and the half v2.6.21 explicitly did not claim.

## Strand E — the two tickets a board unblocks

- **`T-MISTER-DIRECTVIDEO`** — `direct_video` appears nowhere in `emu.sv`. One
  assignment; the acceptance is a picture on a CRT. The SS1 has an integrated
  24-bit ADV7125 and an FPGA YC encoder, so this is testable without an I/O
  board. Landing it unverified would repeat v2.6.6's `VGA_SL`, which advertised
  two OSD options that did nothing.
- **`T-MISTER-MENUMASK`** — the value half is closed and gated; what is left is
  whether the greyed entry *renders*. Eyes on an OSD.

## Strand F — re-measure the incumbent, then decide

The recorded risk is that `NES_MiSTer` scores ~121/125 on AccuracyCoin where
real Famicom AV hardware also scores ~121/125, so there is no published accuracy
headroom and **the core may be declined as a duplicate**. That risk got worse
while v2.6.18–v2.6.21 were being written: `MiSTer-devel/NES_MiSTer` took a burst
of AccuracyCoin-driven commits on **2026-09-15/16** — `$2004`/`$2007` during
rendering, sprite fetch and evaluation across rendering toggles, DMC DMA bus
conflicts and the CPU internal data bus — which are the entries this line just
closed.

So, in order:

1. **Build the current `NES_MiSTer` and run the same AccuracyCoin build against
   it.** Never compare across catalog versions: 121/125, 129, 144 and 149 are
   different corpora and mixing them is this project's own documented trap.
   `README.md:96`'s "121/125" is stale on its face.
2. **Write the feature delta honestly.** It has save states, cheats, palettes,
   Four Score, Zapper, PAL. We have six mapper families and none of those.
   `docs/submission-case.md` currently argues accuracy alone.
3. **Then decide.** If the accuracy case no longer separates the two cores, the
   argument that does is the **co-simulation apparatus** — which the
   contribution wiki asks for by name on AI-generated code (*"evidence of
   quality and accuracy testing"*) and which no incumbent can show.

**The email is a step inside this release, gated on 1–3, not an assumption of
it.** Sending it before the re-measurement would be submitting on a number we
have not checked since the incumbent moved.

## Rung 6 closes on ONE board, and the record says so

The maintainer decision is that rung 6 closes on the SuperStation One alone. The
**two-board claim is re-scoped out and stays open**, labelled, with its reason:
only one board is in hand. Precedent is v2.6.9, which refused to declare a rung
closed on four boards out of five and said so at the time.

**The checklist box cannot be ticked as written.** It reads *"Runs on real
hardware, **both boards**, one `.rbf`"*. Either it is split into a board-specific
tick plus an open two-board row, or it stays unticked with the re-scope as its
reason. It must not be ticked on one board while its text claims two.

**The anchors that become false on first boot**, located by content rather than
by a remembered line number — three of the six this plan first cited were wrong,
which is the laundering shape `AGENTS.md` warns about, committed inside a plan
whose subject is measuring things properly:

| anchor | what it says today |
|---|---|
| `README.md:18` | "**no hardware has run this**, so timing closure and a produced `.rbf` are …" |
| `docs/bringup.md:4` | "**Status: NOT RUN.** No DE10-Nano and no SuperStation One has been attached…" |
| `docs/bringup.md:122` | "every release body says that no hardware has run the bitstream" |
| `docs/submission-case.md:115` | "**No hardware has run this bitstream.** A booting core, a synced display…" |

Plus every release body's closing disclosure. Re-locate them with

time rather than trusting this table — it will have drifted by then, which is

**And the stale incumbent figure is at `README.md:108` and
`docs/submission-case.md:20-21`**, not where the earlier round put it. Both say
121/125 against real Famicom AV hardware's ~121/125; both are pre-2026-09-15 and
are exactly what Strand F re-measures. `README.md:120` already warns against
comparing 121/125, 129, 144 and 149 across corpus versions — so the document
contains both the stale number and the rule against trusting it.

## Risks

- **Declined as a duplicate.** Accepted in writing since v2.4.1. Retro Remake is
  the planned fallback home, not a contingency.
- **A platform fault read as a core fault.** Mitigated by §2.6's baseline on the
  *stock* core before ours is ever loaded.
- **The four properties are the least-verified part of the design and are being
  measured for the first time.** A palette or video-timing correction at this
  stage is an RTL change, which re-opens the seed sweep and the bitstream — so
  budget for a second compile cycle rather than treating bring-up as read-only.
- **`misterfpga.org` returns HTTP 403 to automated fetching**, so its
  Development-forum threads need reading by hand.

## Verification bar

- Every bring-up step carries its artefact; no step is recorded as passed on
  "it looked fine".
- The four properties each have a number, not an adjective.
- Tier 1 is read back as bytes wherever the ROM writes to `$6000`; where it does
  not, the limit is stated rather than papered over.
- Tier 2 is against stock `NES_MiSTer` **on the same silicon**, not against a
  remembered figure.
- Any RTL change re-runs the full ladder, the four-corner timing read and the
  ten-seed sweep. An RTL change invalidates every downstream measurement,
  including ones that look topically unrelated.
- The incumbent is re-measured on the same corpus **before** the submission
  decision, and the numbers for both are published together.
